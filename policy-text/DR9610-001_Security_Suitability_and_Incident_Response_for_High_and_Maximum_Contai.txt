1. PURPOSE

     a.   This Departmental Regulation (DR) establishes United States Department of Agriculture
          (USDA) policy to secure pathogens held at USDA high and maximum containment
          facilities and facilities holding regulated biological select agents and toxins (BSAT).
          The details of implementation of this policy are in USDA, Departmental Manual, (DM)
          9610-001, Security, Suitability, and Incident Response for High and Maximum
          Containment Facilities Procedures.

     b.   Security of pathogens held at non-high and maximum containment facilities is covered
          in another technical facility security manual USDA, DM 9610-002, Security Policies
          and Procedures for Laboratories and Technical Facilities (Excluding Biosafety Level
          (BSL)-3 Facilities).

     c.   In the near future DM 9610-002 will be updated to be consistent with this directive.
  d.   This directive aligns with other USDA safety, physical security, incident response, and
       information security policies, and meets the requirements of the following policy
       documents:

       (1) 9 Code of Federal Regulations (CFR) § 121, Possession, Use, and Transfer of
           Select Agents and Toxins;

       (2) 7 CFR § 331, Possession, Use, and Transfer of Select Agents and Toxins;

       (3) 42 CFR § 73, Select Agents and Toxins;

       (4) Department of Homeland Security (DHS), Cybersecurity & Infrastructure Security
           Agency (CISA), Interagency Security Committee (ISC) Standard, The Risk
           Management Process for Federal Facilities, 2nd Edition; and

       (5) Public Law (P.L.) 107-56, Uniting and Strengthening America by Providing
           Appropriate Tools Required to Intercept and Obstruct Terrorism (USA PATRIOT
           Act).


2. SPECIAL INSTRUCTIONS/CANCELLATIONS

  This directive is effective upon publication and remains in effect until it is superseded or
  expires.


3. SCOPE

  a.   This policy applies to all high containment laboratories, employees, and contractors
       working for or on behalf of USDA.

  b.   This DR contains a uniform set of USDA policies which are intended to cover USDA
       laboratories that work with or have the capacity to work with pathogens requiring high
       containment (i.e., High Consequence Biological Agents (HCBA)) and BSAT. Each
       high and maximum containment facility will create or modify an existing plan for
       physical security that is distinct from their own biorisk management or incident response
       plans. The plans must adhere to the Centers for Disease, Control, and Prevention
       (CDC), Security Plan Guidance, 42 CFR § 73.11, 7 CFR § 331.11, and 9 CFR § 121.
       11.

  c.   The USDA conducts research and regulatory activities, including detection and
       diagnosis, to protect American agriculture, forestry, and human health from pests and
       pathogens. USDA scientists utilize pathogens in their research and diagnostic activities
       that could constitute a threat to either human, animal, and plant health, or productivity of
       the agriculture system if purposely or inadvertently released into the environment.

  d.   The policies in this DR are subject to periodic review as directed by USDA policy, or
       more frequently if conditions warrant earlier review.



4. BACKGROUND

  a.   This policy will define USDA requirements to secure BSAT or other critical HCBA.

  b.   The USDA agencies and staff offices, centers, and laboratories foster a culture of safety.
       All USDA employees and contractors at biocontainment facilities are ultimately
       responsible for safety and security. The USDA employs biorisk management, safety,
       and security professionals as technical resources who are responsible for managing and
       directing the safety and security program(s) of USDA biocontainment laboratories.
       Supervisors and managers at all levels within USDA are responsible for implementing
       operational safety and security programs, with direct oversight assigned to researchers
       and diagnosticians.


5. POLICY

  a.   Ensure appropriate levels of protection against unauthorized access, theft, diversion, loss
       of custody of biologically hazardous agents, or BSAT through the use of graded
       protection and in accordance with the potential consequences of release and based on a
       site-specific physical security risk assessment. Security program elements must include:

       (1) Materials inventory and accountability;

       (2) Physical security;

       (3) Cybersecurity;

       (4) Personnel suitability and insider threat awareness;

       (5) Incident response;

       (6) Training and appropriate follow-up activities; and

       (7) Likelihood and consequences of release.

  b.   Ensure alignment of those entities that possess, use, or transfer BSAT with applicable
       regulations (7 CFR Part 331 (Plant); 9 CFR Part 121 (Animal); and 42 CFR Part 73
       (Human)), and best practices in biological safety, industrial hygiene, physical security,
       and biorisk management.

  c.   Ensure specialized equipment compliance with Section 508 accessibility requirements
       by forwarding all purchases to the USDA Office of the Chief Information Officer
       (OCIO). See DR 4030-001, Section 508 Program.


6. ROLES AND RESPONSIBILITIES

  a.   The USDA Secretary, or their designated responsible staff will:

       (1) Develop, publish, and actively maintain policies, regulations, and compliance
           requirements for safety, biorisk management, biosecurity, physical security,
           incident response, personnel suitability, or information security, including actively
           providing channels for agency and staff office input into and approval of the same;
           and

       (2) Provide management and oversight activities as required to ensure effective safety,
           biorisk management, physical security, incident response, personnel suitability, and
           information security program implementation:

           (a) Reviewing and monitoring compliance to established policy requirements and
               standards with minimal disruption to business functions or mission
               requirements; and

           (b) Reporting compliance and deviations per Office of Management and Budget
               (OMB) requirements.

  b.   Agency and staff officials will:

       (1) Develop and implement internal procedures and controls in support of the
           requirements and standards of this policy;

       (2) Establish effective communication between internal stakeholders and an identified
           Point of Contact (POC) in the Department, agencies, and staff offices;

       (3) Incorporate the policies, requirements, and standards into agency and staff office
           capital planning and investment control processes;

       (4) Provide resources for appropriate training programs, including developing tools
           (e.g., training modules) for use by location staff to institute the mandated training
           programs; and

       (5) Direct random reviews of materials accountability records.

  c.   Center Directors, Laboratory Chiefs or Directors, and Research Leaders, or their
       delegated responsible staff (as appropriate) will:




                                                4
     (1) Individually or collectively ensure effective safety, biorisk management, security,
         and incident response implementation at their facility or location, including access
         control;

     (2) Provide resources for implementation of a robust safety and security program,
         including, but not limited to, training on, and monitoring of, safety and security
         policies and programs;

     (3) Determine and document the level of competency of trained individuals on a
         regular basis and institute additional training as needed;

     (4) Maintain a current, centralized master database for materials accountability;

     (5) Ensure applicable Departmental, agency, staff office, and required regulatory
         reporting requirements are completed in a timely fashion;

     (6) Assume or delegate the role of Incident Commander when necessary, consistent
         with Homeland Security Presidential Directive (HSPD)-5, Management of
         Domestic Incidents, and HSPD-8, National Preparedness; and

     (7) Conduct drills or exercises on a regular basis to test and evaluate the effectiveness
         of the security plan. Organizations shall document the results of the exercise, to
         include procedures confirmed or problems identified. Refer to the CDC, Drills and
         Exercises Guidance, 7 CFR § 331.11, 9 CFR § 121.11, 42 CFR § 73.11 (Security),
         7 CFR § 331.12, 9 CFR § 121.12, 42 CFR § 73.12 (Biosafety), 7 CFR § 331.14, 9
         CFR § 121.142, 42 CRF § 73.14 (Incident Response).

d.   Principal Investigators, Lead Scientists, or their delegated responsible staff as
     appropriate will:

     (1) Individually and collectively ensure effective safety, biorisk management,
         personnel suitability, physical security, incident response, and information security
         implementation at their facility or location;

     (2) Maintain inventory and materials accountability records; and

     (3) Ensure applicable Departmental, agency, staff office, and other required regulatory
         reporting requirements are completed in a timely fashion.

e.   All location staff with programmatic duties for safety or security such as Location
     Biorisk Managers, Quarantine Officers, Collateral Duty Safety Officers, Personnel
     Suitability (Human Resources) Specialists, Physical Security Specialists, Information
     Management Directors, and System Administrators will:




                                              5
       (1) Individually or collectively ensure effective safety, biorisk management, personnel
           suitability, physical security, incident response, and information security
           implementation at their facility or location;

       (2) Work with local line managers to ensure laboratories are adhering to Departmental,
           agency, staff office, and site policy on safety, biorisk management, personnel
           suitability, physical security, incident response, and information security programs;
           and

       (3) Serve as a resource for safety, biorisk management, personnel suitability, physical
           security, incident response, and information security program implementation,
           quality control, inspections, and training.

  f.   All USDA staff and contractors will:

       (1) Actively participate as appropriate to ensure effective safety, biorisk management,
           personnel suitability, physical security, incident response, and information security
           implementation at their facility or location; and

       (2) Work with local line managers as appropriate to support their location’s adherence
           to Departmental, agency, staff office, and site policy on safety, biorisk
           management, biosecurity, personnel suitability, physical security, incident response,
           and information security programs.


7. POLICY EXCEPTIONS

  a.   All USDA agencies and staff offices must conform to this policy; however, in the event
       that a specific policy requirement cannot be met as explicitly stated, agencies and staff
       offices must submit a waiver request. The waiver request must explain the reason for
       the request, identify mitigating controls or actions that meet the intent of the policy, and
       identify how the mitigating controls or actions provide a similar or greater level of safety
       and security or compliance than the policy requirement.

  b.   Requests for waivers will be considered on a case-by-case basis, and in the majority of
       cases, can be resolved between the field and staff levels. Agencies and staff offices
       should address all policy waiver request memorandums to their individual safety, biorisk
       management, physical security, incident response, personnel security, or cybersecurity
       POCs as appropriate.

  c.   Unless otherwise specified, agencies and staff offices must review and renew approved
       policy waivers every fiscal year. Approved waivers must be tracked as a plan of action
       and milestones item if the mitigation proposed was working toward policy compliance
       over time. When completed and instituted, approved waivers for specialized equipment
       and facilities requirements or enhancements with a continuing need will simply be
       tracked on an approved list.



                                                6
  d.   The appropriate agency or staff office-level office must monitor and approve waivers to
       this policy internally. Consultation with additional Departmental resources or subject
       matter experts on changing conditions as waivers are monitored will be pursued on a
       case-by-case basis.

  e.   The written waiver request (request for exception) must be in the form of a decision
       memorandum and must include:

       (1) Being addressed to the appropriate agency or staff office resource (e.g., Associate
           Administrator of National Programs, Associate Administrator of Research
           Operations, etc.) through the lead security professional (Director level);

       (2) Name of submitting agency or staff office;

       (3) Name, contact information, signature of submitting person; and

       (4) Description of the policy exception requested:

           (a) Justification to show good cause for the exception. The request should
               document the justifications for the exception; and

           (b) The impact of granting versus not granting the request.


8. INQUIRIES

  Address inquiries concerning this DR to the Agriculture Research Service (ARS) Homeland
  Security Division (HSD), via email at, ARSHomelandSecurityDivision@usda.gov mailbox.
