1. PURPOSE

     This Departmental Regulation (DR) establishes the United States Department of Agriculture
     (USDA) policy for protecting human health and the environment through pollution
     prevention, control, and abatement measures. The DR also establishes policy to minimize
     USDA’s potential liability by ensuring environmental cleanup and compliance with
     applicable Federal, State and local laws. This DR does not establish policy related to
     requirements under the National Environmental Policy Act (NEPA) or environmental
     regulations related to natural and cultural resource management, except as those apply to
     environmental response and cleanup actions. Appendix C, Authorities and References,
     contains a list of key Federal environmental laws, regulations, and executive orders. All
     States and many localities have related laws and ordinances.


2. SPECIAL INSTRUCTIONS/CANCELLATIONS

     a.   This DR, along with DR 5600-006, Sustainable Operations, supersedes DR 5600-005,
          Environmental Management, dated September 24, 2012.
  b.   Procedures related to this DR are provided in Departmental Manual (DM) 5600-001,
       Environmental Pollution Prevention, Control, and Abatement Manual.


3. SCOPE

  This DR applies to all USDA Mission Areas, agencies, and staff offices:

  a.   It applies to all USDA facilities and properties, owned or leased (hereafter referred to as
       USDA properties); and all activities conducted on USDA properties.

  b.   It applies to all activities performed by USDA’s employees and appointees.

  c.   It applies to activities performed by USDA’s contractors, permittees, grantees, and
       concession partners, when they are conducting those activities on USDA properties.

  d.   It applies to activities performed on non-USDA properties by USDA’s contractors when
       conducting or supporting environmental response or compliance actions under contract
       to a Mission Area, agency, or staff office.


4. POLICY

  USDA’s environmental management policy is as follows:

  a.   General

       (1) Protect human health and the environment.

       (2) Comply with all applicable Federal, State, and local environmental laws and
           requirements, including, but not limited to, the laws, regulations, and executive
           orders listed in Appendix C.

       (3) Respond to and clean up spills and releases and restore the environment.

       (4) Consider disaster resilience and sustainable practices in all actions and implement
           those practices when feasible.

       (5) Establish and follow procedures to monitor and improve environmental compliance.

       (6) Prepare Environmental Compliance Plans (ECP) to monitor and improve
           environmental compliance that include:

            (a) Training requirements for USDA staff with environmental duties;

            (b) An audit plan and schedule; and



                                               2
         (c) Procedures for tracking audit findings, notices of violation (NOV), other
             notices of noncompliance, and the corrective actions to resolve them.

     (7) Conduct environmental compliance audits at least once every 5 years at Mission
         Areas, agencies, and staff offices that store chemicals in facilities.

     (8) Use Environmental Management Systems (EMS) where appropriate. Implement
         and maintain effective management tools.

     (9) Train staff in environmental compliance and response.

b.   Environmental Preparedness and Emergency Response

     (1) Partner with Federal, State, and local agencies on the National Response Team
         (NRT) and Regional Response Teams (RRT).

     (2) Support Federal, State, regional, and local efforts to plan for, respond to, and
         recover from oil spills and hazardous substance releases.

     (3) Conduct natural resource damage assessments and remediate and restore impacted
         resources. Seek reimbursement from the potentially responsible parties (PRP).

     (4) For oil spills to the waters of the United States, seek reimbursement for all relevant
         activities and natural resource damages from the PRP. For costs not reimbursed by
         a PRP, seek reimbursement from the Oil Spill Liability Trust Fund (OSLTF).

     (5) Train USDA employees in preparedness and emergency response activities.

c.   Environmental Cleanup

     (1) Maintain a USDA-wide inventory of potentially contaminated sites and track
         progress in addressing the sites identified as contaminated.

     (2) Fund cleanup response actions.

     (3) Perform investigations and cleanups or oversee cleanups using USDA’s authority
         under the Comprehensive Environmental Response, Compensation, and Liability
         Act (CERCLA), as amended.

     (4) Perform investigations and cleanups under the Resource Conservation and
         Recovery Act of 1976 (RCRA), as amended, 42 United States Code (U.S.C.) § 6901
         or other state regulatory programs, when required by state or Federal regulatory
         authorities.

     (5) Use interim remedies where appropriate to manage risk.



                                             3
     (6) Consider agency-determined natural resource and land use goals when selecting
         remedies.

     (7) Prefer remedies that remove all waste and contaminants to minimize restrictions on
         land use.

     (8) Ensure PRPs perform cleanups or pay for USDA’s cleanup costs.

     (9) When other entities, including Federal agencies, perform response actions on
         USDA land or facilities:

         (a) Use formal agreements that govern access to the land for assessment and
             response actions;

         (b) Review plans and decisions to ensure all proposed actions meet USDA’s
             policies and requirements; and

         (c) Use formal post-remediation operations and maintenance agreements for
             remedies that leave waste in place to document the land use restrictions and
             responsibilities of each party in maintaining controls.

     (10) Assess natural resource damages and seek cost recovery from PRPs.

     (11) Train USDA employees conducting environmental response actions.

d.   Environmental Liability

     (1) Perform environmental site assessments (ESA) and other environmental due
         diligence for all real property acquisitions including the purchase, lease, transfer,
         exchange, donation, or gift of real property.

     (2) Include notification of hazardous substance activity and all other necessary
         environmental information in the title report for all real property disposals, as
         appropriate.

     (3) Minimize liability and financial risk related to real property acquisitions and
         conveyances.

     (4) Support community revitalization efforts for brownfields, when appropriate.

     (5) Prohibit the placement of new landfills on USDA land.

5. ROLES AND RESPONSIBILITIES

  a.   The Assistant Secretary for Administration will oversee the USDA’s environmental
       management program.

  b.   The USDA Sustainable Operations Council will advise USDA’s environmental
       management programs (see DR 1058-001, Sustainable Operations Council).

  c.   The Director of the Office of Property and Environmental Management (OPEM) will:

       (1) Develop and implement policy, standards, procedures, and guidance for:

           (a) Environmental compliance;

           (b) Preventing, controlling, and abating pollution;

           (c) Waste management;

           (d) Management of oils, hazardous substances, pollutants, and contaminants;

           (e) Environmental due diligence for real property transactions;

           (f) EMS; and

           (g) Environmental program training for USDA managers and employees;

       (2) Represent USDA on interagency teams and in responding to requests regarding
           USDA’s environmental programs, including:

           (a) Consult or work with other Federal agencies to develop policies for Federal
               facilities’ compliance with applicable pollution control laws, including:

               1    U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (EPA);

               2    White House’s Council on Environmental Quality; and

               3    Office of Management and Budget;

           (b) Represent USDA on the NRT:

               1    Participate in NRT member and subcommittee meetings;

               2    Lead USDA planning and preparedness activities; and

               3    Coordinate support for emergency spill response, when the NRT is
                    activated;



                                              5
    (c) Coordinate USDA support to the Federal Emergency Management Agency
        (FEMA) through Emergency Support Function (ESF) #10, Oil and Hazardous
        Materials Response, when ESF#10 is activated;

    (d) Coordinate USDA support for Brownfields redevelopment; and

    (e) Coordinate, prepare, and submit USDA-wide reports to oversight entities;

(3) Manage resources and provide support to the Mission Area, agency, and staff office
    environmental programs, including:

    (a) Prepare an annual budget request for program funding needs;

    (b) Manage the Hazardous Materials Management Appropriation (HMMA);

    (c) Manage the USDA Forest Service New World Mine Response and Restoration
        account pursuant to Public Law 105-83 Section 502, Department of the Interior
        and Related Agencies Appropriations Act, 1998, Protection and Preservation
        of Yellowstone National Park-Acquisition of Crown Butte Mining Interests;

    (d) Maintain a USDA-wide inventory of potentially contaminated sites and track
        progress in addressing those sites identified as contaminated. These sites are
        currently recorded in the National Environmental Accomplishment Tracking
        (NEAT) system;

    (e) Support the RRT members in planning for and responding to oil spills and
        releases of hazardous substances, pollutants, and contaminants; and

    (f) Take actions as may be necessary to require response action under CERCLA
        Sections 106(a) and 122 (except subsection (b)(1)), with the affected agency
        head and with the concurrence of the USDA Office of the General Counsel
        (OGC). These actions include issuing administrative orders and agreements
        with any person or entity;

(4) Monitor Mission Area, agency, and staff office environmental programs and work
    with them to improve identified deficiencies, including:

    (a) Monitor CERCLA and RCRA cleanup projects;

    (b) Monitor enforcement and track cost recovery efforts from PRPs;

    (c) Oversee resolution of NOVs;

    (d) Review agency ESA reports in compliance with EPA’s All Appropriate
        Inquiries (AAI) Rule found in 42 United States Code (U.S.C) § 9601

d.   USDA Mission Area, Agency, or Staff Office Heads, as appropriate, will:

     (1) Ensure Mission Area, agency, and staff offices comply with pollution control laws,

     (2) Involve all relevant staff in USDA’s environmental management programs;

     (3) Assign an Environmental Program Manager to lead reporting and compliance;

     (4) Assign Remedial Project Managers (RPM) and On-Scene Coordinators (OSC) to
         direct response efforts for releases of hazardous substances, pollutants, or
         contaminants. The responsibilities for these positions are specified in 40 Code of
         Federal Regulations (CFR) § 300.120, National Oil and Hazardous Substances
         Pollution Contingency Plan (NCP), On-scene coordinators and remedial project
         managers: general responsibilities;

         out responsibilities, in accordance with 40 CFR § 300.120;

     (6) Fund cleanup response actions;

     (7) Identify PRPs and recover costs to pay for spill cleanup and response actions on
         USDA property;

     (8) Pursue PRPs to pay for natural resource damages on USDA property;

(9) Recover eligible costs from the OSLTF for oil spill response activities and natural
    resource damages that are not reimbursed by a PRP;

(10) Prepare ESA reports compliant with EPA’s AAI rule (acquisitions) and provide
     notifications of hazardous substance activity in the title reports (disposals) for real
     property acquisitions and disposals;

(11) Maintain a cumulative inventory of:

    (a) Spills of oil and hazardous substances above reportable quantities;

    (b) Locations on USDA-managed lands where hazardous substances have been
        stored for at least 1 year; and

    (c) Potential or known contaminated sites, resulting from historic releases;

(12) Coordinate with OGC when:

    (a) Pursuing PRPs to recover costs; and

    (b) Preparing ESA reports and notifications of hazardous substance activity for
        real property acquisitions and disposals;

(13) Notify OPEM and OGC in writing of the following occurrences:

        burns;

        USDA lands; and

    (d) When entering into a partnership that results in a joint or sole responsibility for
        long term maintenance and operation of hazardous substances, or waste
        disposal sites (e.g., waste repositories or waste-water treatment systems);

(14) Submit for OPEM and OGC review ESA reports on proposed USDA loan
     foreclosures of commercial property, farms, and other non-residential property;

(15) Submit for OPEM and OGC review and OPEM approval, as also required in the DR
     5100-004:

     (1) Represent USDA on each of the 13 RRTs and fund expenses incurred by the USDA

     (3) Provide support to FEMA through ESF #10, Oil and Hazardous Materials

     (4) Coordinate with the USDA Mission Areas, agencies, and staff offices to provide

         and

     (5) Track costs of USDA’s response actions for reimbursement by the OSLTF or the
         potentially responsible party.

     (1) Support the head of their Mission Area, agency, or staff office, as appropriate, in

     (3) Create and maintain an ECP(s);

     (4) Implement compliance review and audit procedures;

     (5) Track and correct audit findings;

     (6) Evaluate any NOVs and develop and implement corrective actions;

     (7) Create EMSs at appropriate locations or facilities; and

           USDA’s inventory management system, currently NEAT.

  g.   The USDA General Counsel will:

       (1) Assist OPEM and USDA Mission Areas, agencies, and staff offices with response,
           enforcement, and cost recovery activities;

       (2) Provide legal review for proposed real property transactions involving known or
           suspected contamination;

       (3) Provide legal review for proposed USDA loan foreclosures involving known or
           suspected contamination; and

       (4) Advise OPEM and Mission Areas, agencies, and staff offices on environmental
           compliance requirements and activities.

                                        DEFINITIONS

Agency. Organizational units of the Department, other than staff offices as defined below,
whose heads report to officials within the Office of the Secretary, Deputy Secretary, Under
Secretaries, Assistant Secretaries, and Assistant to the Secretary. (Source: USDA, DR 0100-
001, Departmental Directives System)

All Appropriate Inquiries. An inquiry into the previous ownership and uses of a facility in
accordance with generally accepted good commercial and customary standards and practices,
that are codified in 40 CFR § 312, Innocent Land Owners, Standards for Conducting All
Appropriate Inquiries. (Source: 42 U.S.C. § 9601(35)(B)(i)(I), Hazardous Substances Releases,
Liabilities, and Compensation, Definitions)

Brownfields. A “brownfield site” means real property, the expansion, redevelopment, or reuse
of which may be complicated by the presence or potential presence of a hazardous substance,
pollutant, or contaminant. (Source: 42 U.S.C. § 9601(39)(A), Hazardous Substances Releases,
Liabilities, and Compensation, Brownfield Site)

Environmental Management System (EMS). Part of an organization's management system used
to develop and implement its environmental policy and manage its activities, products, or
services that can interact with the environment. (Source: International Organization for
Standardization, International Standard ISO 14001:2015, Environmental Management Systems –
Requirements for Guidance and Use)

Environmental Program Manager. The single Mission Area, agency, or staff office point of
contact who leads the implementation of environmental regulations, DR 5100-004, Real
Property Management, and reporting activities on behalf of the Mission Area, agency(ies), or
staff office under their assignment.

Hazardous Substance. Elements, compounds, and wastes designated in Table 302.4—List of
hazardous substances and reportable quantities. (Source: 40 CFR § 302.4, NCP, Designations
of Hazardous Substances)

Mission Area. A group of agencies with related functions that report to the same Under or
Assistant Secretary. Research, Education, and Economics is an example of a Mission Area.
(Source: USDA, DR 0100-001)

Notice of Violation (NOV). Any written notice from a Federal, State, or local entity with
authority to enforce environmental laws and regulations to a Mission Area, agency, or staff
office stating they have violated an environmental law or other requirement.

Oil. Oil of any kind or in any form, including petroleum, fuel oil, sludge, oil refuse, and oil
mixed with wastes other than dredged spoil, but does not include any substance which is
specifically listed or designated as a hazardous substance under subparagraphs (A) through (F) of



                                              B-1
Section 101(14) of CERCLA of 1980 and which is subject to the provisions of that Act. (Source:
33 U.S.C § 2701, Oil Pollution Liability and Compensation)

Oil Spill. Also, oil discharge. An occurrence or series of occurrences having the same origin,
involving one or more vessels, facilities, or any combination thereof, resulting in the discharge or
substantial threat of discharge of oil into or upon navigable waters of the United States, adjoining
shorelines, or the exclusive economic zone. (Source: USCG, National Pollution Funds Center
(NPFC) User Reference Guide, USCG NPFC website)

Oil Spill Liability Trust Fund (OSLTF). The fund authorized under the Oil Pollution Act of 1990
(OPA), 33 U.S.C. § 2701, et seq. to pay for expeditious oil removal and uncompensated damages
from oil spills. The USCG's NPFC is responsible for managing and administering the Fund,
including adjudicating claims. (Source: USCG, NPFC User Reference Guide, USCG NPFC
website)

Pollutant or contaminant. Any element, substance, compound, mixture, or disease-causing
agents defined by Section 101(33) of CERCLA of 1980. (Source: 40 CFR § 300.5, NCP,
Definitions)

Potentially responsible party (PRP). Any person who may be liable pursuant to Section 107(a)
of CERCLA of 1980, for response costs incurred and to be incurred by the United States not
inconsistent with the NCP. (Source: 40 CFR § 304.12, Arbitration Procedures for Small
Superfund Cost Recovery Claims)

Real Property. Means land, including land improvements, structures and appurtenances thereto,
but excludes moveable machinery and equipment. (Source: 41 CFR § 105-72.101, General
Definitions)

Release. Any spilling, leaking, pumping, pouring, emitting, emptying, discharging, injecting,
escaping, leaching, dumping, or disposing into the environment (including the abandonment or
discarding of barrels, containers, and other closed receptacles) of any toxic chemical. (Source:
40 CFR § 372.3, Toxic Chemical Release Reporting: Community Right-To-Know)

Staff Office. Departmental administrative offices whose heads report to officials within the
Office of the Secretary. (Source: USDA, DR 0100-001)

Waters of the United States. Defined in Water Programs, Definition of Waters of the United
States, 40 CFR § 120.2 et seq.
