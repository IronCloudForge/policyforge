1. PURPOSE

     This Departmental Regulation (DR) establishes the processes required to develop the United
     States Department of Agriculture (USDA) Information Technology (IT) Strategic Plan in
     accordance with:

     a.   The Clinger-Cohen Act of 1996, 40 United States Code (U.S.C.) Section 11101, et seq.;

     b.   The Government Performance and Results Modernization Act of 2010, Public Law
          (P.L.) § 111-352;

     c.   The Federal Information Technology Acquisition Reform Act (FITARA), P.L. 113-291
          §§ 831-837;

     d.   The Paperwork Reduction Act of 1995, 44 U.S.C. § 3501, et seq.;

     e.   Federal Agency Responsibilities, 44 U.S.C. § 3506(b)(2);
  f.   Performance and Results-based Management, 40 U.S.C. § 11313;

  g.   Chief Information Officer, 7 Code of Federal Regulations (CFR) § 2.32;

  h.   Office of Management and Budget (OMB), Circular A-11 Revised, Preparing,
       Submitting, and Executing the Budget;

  i.   OMB, Circular A-130, Management of Federal Information Resources; and

  j.   OMB, Memorandum M-15-14, Management and Oversight of Federal Information
       Technology.


2. SPECIAL INSTRUCTIONS/CANCELLATIONS

  This DR supersedes DR 3111-001, Departmental Long-Range IRM Planning, dated February
  2, 1989.


3. SCOPE

  This DR applies to all USDA Mission Areas, agencies, and staff offices.


4. BACKGROUND

  The USDA Information Resources Management (IRM) Strategic Plan details the vision and
  goals for USDA to improve its acquiring and managing information resources. The USDA
  IRM Strategic Plan, also known as the USDA IT Strategic Plan, should support the USDA
  Strategic Plan. A current approved USDA IT Strategic Plan should be in place when
  approving any proposed major IT acquisition.


5. POLICY

  a.   The USDA IT Strategic Plan will be prepared by the Office of the Chief Information
       Officer (OCIO).

  b.   Per the Government Performance and Results Modernization Act of 2010, the drafting of
       strategic plans is an inherently Governmental function and will be performed only by
       Federal employees.

  c.   For each new Presidential term, OCIO will issue a USDA IT Strategic Plan covering a
       period of not less than 4 years. The USDA IT Strategic Plan will be made available on

       the USDA public website.

  d.   The USDA IT Strategic Plan will align with the USDA Strategic Plan called for in
       Government Performance and Results Modernization Act of 2010, A-130, and A-11.


6. ROLES AND RESPONSIBILITIES

  a.   The USDA Secretary will ensure that the Chief Information Officer (CIO) has a
       significant role in:

       (1) The decision processes for annual multi-year planning, programming, budgeting,
           and execution decisions, related reporting requirements, and reports related to IT;
           and

       (2) The management, governance, and oversight processes related to IRM.

  b.   The USDA Deputy Secretary, serving as Chief Operating Officer (COO), will approve
       and sign the final USDA IT Strategic Plan.

  c.   The USDA CIO will:

       (1) Serve as the USDA senior official for developing, establishing, executing, and
           maintaining the USDA IT Strategic Plan and planning processes under which it was
           created according to applicable laws and regulations;

       (2) Ensure the USDA IT Strategic Plan directly supports the USDA Strategic Plan and
           overall mission of the USDA;

       (3) Advise the USDA Secretary on all matters pertaining to IT strategic planning;

       (4) Ensure that performance measurements:

           (a) Are prescribed for IT; and

           (b) Measure how well the IT supports programs of USDA (per 40 U.S.C. §
               11313);

       (5) Analyze the missions of USDA. Based on the analysis, revise the mission-related
           processes and administrative processes as appropriate before making significant
           investments in IT to support the performance in those missions (per 40 U.S.C. §
           11313);

       (6) Oversee the development and production of the USDA IT Strategic Plan;

     (7) Establish and maintain a process to regularly engage with program managers to
         evaluate IT-related resources supporting each USDA strategic objective;

     (8) Coordinate with the USDA Chief Financial Officer (CFO), the USDA Chief
         Acquisition Officer (CAO), and the Office of Budget and Program Analysis
         (OBPA) Director to define Departmentwide policy for the level of detail of planned
         expenditure reporting for all transactions that include IT resources;

     (9) Annually assess progress toward achieving the intended outcomes of each IT
         strategic objective; and

     (10) Delegate authorities and responsibilities as necessary.

d.   The OCIO Information Resource Management Center (IRMC) Associate Chief Information
     Officer (ACIO) will:

     (1) Manage, coordinate, produce, and publish the USDA IT Strategic Plan and its
         processes; and

     (2) Prepare Departmental IT strategic planning guidance to Mission Areas, agencies,
         and staff offices to build and update the USDA IT Strategic Plan.

e.   The Assistant Secretary for Administration will:

     (1) Serve as the USDA CAO; and

     (2) Coordinate with the CIO, OBPA Director and the CFO to define Departmentwide
         policy for the level of detail of planned expenditure reporting for all transactions
         that include IT resources.

f.   The USDA CFO will coordinate with the CIO, the OBPA Director, and the CAO to
     define Departmentwide policy for the level of detail of planned expenditure reporting for
     all transactions that include IT resources.

g.   The USDA OBPA Director will coordinate with the CIO, CFO, and the CAO to define
     Departmentwide policy for the level of detail of planned expenditure reporting for all
     transactions that include IT resources.

h.   Mission Area Assistant Chief Information Officers, including the Departmental
     Administration Information Technology Office (DAITO), will:

     (1) Work with the CIO to define IT performance metrics and strategies to support the
         fulfillment of USDA strategic objectives as defined in the USDA Strategic Plan;

     (2) Provide OCIO IT-related updates reflecting the status of their IT investments, goals,
         and objectives compared to the assumptions as defined by the Department in its



                                             4
           USDA IT Strategic Plan; and

      (3) Implement the goals and objectives outlined in the USDA IT Strategic Plan in a
          timely fashion.


7. POLICY EXCEPTIONS

  Mission Areas, agencies, and staff offices may request a temporary exemption from the
  USDA CIO for a single planning cycle in the event of some special organizational situation
  precluding IT strategic planning temporarily.


8. INQUIRIES

  Questions and comments concerning the requirements of this regulation should be directed to
  OCIO-IRMC at SM.OCIO.IRMPolicy@usda.gov.

                                         DEFINITIONS


Information Resources Management. Managing information resources to accomplish agency
missions and improve agency performance, including reducing information collection burdens
on the public. (Source: 44 U.S.C. § 3502)

Information Technology (IT). Information technology means any services or equipment, or
interconnected system(s) or subsystem(s) of equipment, that are used in the automatic
acquisition, storage, analysis, evaluation, manipulation, management, movement, control,
display, switching, interchange, transmission, or reception of data or information by the Agency.
For purposes of this definition, such services or equipment is used by the Agency directly or is
used by a contractor under a contract with the Agency that requires its use; or to a significant
extent, its use in the performance of a service or the furnishing of a product. Information
technology includes computers, ancillary equipment (including imaging peripherals, input,
output, and storage devices necessary for security and surveillance), peripheral equipment
designed to be controlled by the central processing unit of a computer, software, firmware, and
similar procedures, services (including cloud computing and help-desk services or other
professional services which support any point of the life cycle of the equipment or service), and
related resources. The term information technology does not include any equipment acquired by
a contractor incidental to a contract that does not require its use (40 U.S.C. §11101). (Source:
OMB, Circular A-130)
