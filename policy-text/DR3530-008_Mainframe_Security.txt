1. PURPOSE

      This Departmental Regulation (DR) sets the policy for securing mainframes at the United
      States Department of Agriculture (USDA).


2. SCOPE

      a.   This DR applies to all:

           (1) Mission Areas, agencies, and staff offices who own, operate, manage, or control
               mainframes;

           (2) Federal data in any medium or form that the USDA or those on behalf of the USDA
               generates, collects, provides, transmits, stores, maintains, or accesses;
       (3) Systems or services, including cloud-based, that the USDA or those on behalf of the
           USDA use or operate. These include connections between or among data systems or
           services; and

       (4) Facilities from which USDA systems operate whether the USDA or another entity
           on behalf of the USDA uses or operates them.

  b.   Nothing in this policy alters the need to protect national security systems or data.


3. SPECIAL INSTRUCTIONS/CANCELLATIONS

  a.   This DR supersedes Departmental Manual (DM) 3530-002, IBM & IBM Compatible
       Mainframe Security, dated July 15, 2004, in its entirety.

  b.   This DR takes effect immediately when published. It stays in effect until superseded, or
       it expires.

  c.   All USDA Mission Areas, agencies, and staff offices who own or operate mainframes
       will align their mainframe security procedures with this DR within 6 months of its
       publication.

  d.   This policy complies with:

       (1) Committee on National Security Systems policies, directives, and instructions;

       (2) The Federal Information Security Modernization Act of 2014, 44 U.S.C. § 3551, et
           seq.;

       (3) Federal requirements to set, implement, and enforce cybersecurity standards to
           manage risks to USDA mainframe resources;

       (4) Intelligence community policies, directives, and instructions;

       (5) National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST), Federal Information
           Processing Standards Publication (FIPS PUB) 199, Standards for Security
           Categorization of Federal Information and Information Systems;

       (6) NIST, FIPS PUB 200, Minimum Security Requirements for Federal Information
           and Information Systems;

       (7) NIST, Special Publication (SP) 800-37, Revision 2, Risk Management Framework
           for Information Systems and Organizations;

       (8) NIST, SP 800-53, Revision 5, Security and Privacy Controls for Information
           Systems and Organizations;

       (9) NIST, SP 800-53A, Revision 5, Assessing Security and Privacy Controls in
           Information Systems and Organizations; and

4. BACKGROUND

  USDA published DM 3530-002 in 2004. Since then, computing and cybersecurity have
  evolved tremendously. As such, the USDA needed a foundational policy with guidance on
  managing mainframe systems to conduct USDA business activities. This policy serves as that
  baseline, with links to related policies.


5. POLICY

  a.   The Mission Area, agency, or staff office Mainframe System Owner, or designated
       official, will assign a System Security Administrator (SSA) and backup SSAs as
       necessary. There will be at least one SSA or backup SSA at each USDA site operating a
       mainframe.

  b.   The Mission Area, agency, or staff office Authorizing Official (AO) or designee will
       approve or reject SSA and backup SSA appointments.

  c.   Security and system administration are two distinct roles. The USDA security
       management system separates these privileges at the group level. This means the roles
       and tasks must remain split. Therefore, Mission Areas, agencies, and staff offices will
       not assign mainframe security administration to a System Operator, a System or
       Application Programmer, or a System or Database Administrator.

  d.   Security and mainframe system administrators must work closely to maximize security
       and performance.

  e.   Mainframe security feature testing will include system tests and evaluation, such as
       operating system tool functionality and security solution syntax.

  f.   Refer to the following policies related to mainframe security:

       (1) DR 3505-003, Access Control for Information and Information Systems for
           guidance on managing access to mainframe systems;

       (2) DR 3505-005, Cybersecurity Incident Management for guidance on managing
           cyber-related incidents on mainframe systems;

       (3) DR 3520-002, Configuration Management for guidance on configuring mainframe
           systems;

       (4) DR 3530-006, Scanning and Remediation of Configuration and Patch
           Vulnerabilities for guidance on mainframe system vulnerability management;

       (5) DR 3571-001, Information System Contingency Planning and Disaster Recovery
           Planning for guidance on mainframe system contingency and recovery planning;

       (6) DR 3575-002, System and Information Integrity for guidance on managing
           mainframe system data integrity;

       (7) DR 3575-004, Information Technology Security Baselines and Security Control
           Tailoring for guidance on managing mainframe system data integrity; and

       (8) DR 3185-004, Enterprise Zero Trust Architecture for guidance on zero trust
           standards.


6. ROLES AND RESPONSIBILITIES

  a.   The USDA Chief Information Security Officer (CISO) will create, implement, and
       confirm adherence to cybersecurity policies.

  b.   The Cybersecurity Defense Operations Division (CDOD), Cybersecurity Watch Branch
       (CWB) will investigate malicious activity per DR 3505-005.

  c.   The Mission Area Assistant Chief Information Officer with at least one mainframe in
       their purview will:

       (1) Verify that the onsite list of controlled unclassified data systems and applications
           (operational, and under development) undergoes proper maintenance;

       (2) Test the mainframe’s security features:

           (a) Include system tests and evaluation, such as operating system tool functionality
               and security solution syntax;

           (b) Confirm the features work as claimed in the system’s documentation by testing
               at least annually; and

           (c) A third party must conduct the testing. The third party will act independent of
               development testing and the development organization.

  d.   The Mission Area Assistant CISO with at least one mainframe in their purview will:

       (1) Review risk assessments on identified mainframe vulnerabilities; and

       (2) Approve or deny requests to investigate suspicious activity of a targeted user’s data
           files, roles, and account activity.

  e.   AOs or designees will approve or reject the SSA and backup SSA appointment requests.

  f.   Mainframe System Owners or their designees will submit SSA and backup SSA
       appointments to their AO.

                                               4
  g.   The Information System Security Manager (ISSM) with at least one mainframe in their
       purview or their designee will:

       (1) Audit a mainframe user’s activities to compare them with their designated role in
           security management systems, per DR 3505-003;

       (2) Work with CDOD CWB on any confirmed malicious activity, per DR 3505-005;

       (3) Obtain the Mission Area Assistant CISO’s approval before investigating suspicious
           activity of a targeted user’s data files, roles, and account;

       (4) Create and maintain an onsite list of controlled unclassified data systems and
           applications (operational, and in development). The list must contain the name and
           a brief description of the system and each application;

       (5) Develop mainframe audit procedures. Integrate them with OMB-required internal
           control procedures; and

       (6) Write a risk assessment for identified vulnerabilities. Submit it to their Mission
           Area Assistant CISO.

  h.   SSAs will:

       (1) Maintain user, role, group, and resource access for the mainframes in their purview;
           and

       (2) Conduct a daily review of system security violation reports:

           (a) Review any suspicious mainframe activity;

           (b) Perform a preliminary investigation to confirm or rule out malicious origins;
               and

           (c) Refer confirmed malicious activity to CDOD CWB and appropriate Mission
               Area or agency personnel including their ISSM.


7. PENALTIES AND DISCIPLINARY ACTIONS FOR NONCOMPLIANCE

  a.   DR 4070-735-001, Employee Responsibilities and Conduct, Section 16: Computers, sets
       forth USDA policy, procedures, and standards on employee responsibilities and conduct
       regarding the use of computers and telecommunications equipment. In addition, Section
       21: Disciplinary or Adverse Action states:

       (1) Any violation of the responsibilities or standards contained in this DR may be cause
           for disciplinary or adverse action; and

       (2) Any disciplinary or adverse action taken will be consistent with the applicable laws
           and regulations.

                                               5
  b.   Such disciplinary actions will follow applicable laws and regulations. These include
       those from the Office of Personnel Management, OMB, and 5 CFR § 2635, Standards of
       Ethical Conduct for Employees of the Executive Branch.


8. POLICY EXCEPTIONS

  If Mission Areas, agencies, and staff offices cannot meet a specific policy requirement, they
  can request a policy exception. To do so, email the Office of the Chief Information Officer
  (OCIO), Cybersecurity & Privacy Operations Center (CPOC), Security Management
  Division, Risk Management Branch at POAMProgram@usda.gov. Note that an approved
  policy exception is an acceptance of risk but does not equal compliance.


9. INQUIRIES

  Send any questions or concerns about this DR to OCIO CPOC via CPOC-
  CyberPolicy@usda.gov.
