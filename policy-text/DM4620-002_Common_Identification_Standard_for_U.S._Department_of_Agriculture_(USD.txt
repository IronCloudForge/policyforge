14   1. PURPOSE
15
16      a.   This Departmental Manual (DM) provides procedures for United States Department of
17           Agriculture (USDA) staff to meet the requirements of the Departmental Regulation
18           (DR) 4620-002, Common Identification Standard for U.S. Department of Agriculture.
19
20
21   2. SCOPE
22
23      a.   This DM applies to all USDA Mission Areas, agencies, and staff offices including the
24           employees (as defined in 5 U.S.C. § 2105, Employee) and other categories of persons
25           such as affiliates, contractors, fellows, interns, vendors, and volunteers who have a
26           relationship with USDA under a contract, grant, or other agreement within them. For the
27           remainder of this DM the other categories of persons who have a relationship with
28           USDA under a contract, grant, or other agreement will be referred to collectively as
29           “non-Federal employees”.

 8   4. BACKGROUND
 9
10     Prior to the inception of HSPD-12, federal agencies used a wide range of methods to
11     credential and authenticate federal employees and contractors as a requirement to enter
12     federally controlled facilities and information systems. HSPD-12 required a Governmentwide
13     Standard for secure and reliable forms of identification (ID) issued to Federal employees and
14     contractors.
15
16     This DM provides procedures to keep USDA in compliance with federal policy and
17     standards regarding the credentialing of Federal and non-Federal employees. The primary
18     policies and standards to support the procedures in this DM are:
19
20     a.   Homeland Security Presidential Directive 12 (HSPD)-12, Policy for a Common
21          Identification Standard for Federal Employees and Contractors, which mandated the
22          development and implementation of a mandatory, Governmentwide Standard for secure
23          and reliable forms of identification (ID) issued to Federal employees and contractors.
24          HSPD-12 compliant identification:
25
26          (1) Is issued based on sound criteria for verifying an individual employee's identity;
27
28          (2) Is strongly resistant to identity fraud, tampering, counterfeiting, and exploitation;
29
30          (3) Can be rapidly authenticated electronically; and
31
32          (4) Is issued only by providers whose reliability has been established by an official
33              accreditation process.
34
35     b.   The National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) Federal Information
36          Processing Standards Publication (FIPS PUB) 201-3, Personal Identity Verification
37          (PIV) of Federal Employees and Contractors which:
38
39          (1) Defines a reliable, Governmentwide PIV system for use in applications such as
40              access to federally controlled facilities or information systems;
41
42          (2) Specifies a PIV system within which common identification badges can be created
43              and later used to verify a claimed identity; and
44
45          (3) Requires identity proofing and background investigations (BI) to verify identity.
46

14   5. PRIVACY POLICY
15
16     HSPD-12 explicitly states that “protect[ing] personal privacy” is a requirement of the PIV
17     system. As a USAccess customer, USDA’s PIV process and system is in accordance with
18     the spirit and letter of all privacy controls specified in FIPS PUB 201-3, as well as those
19     specified in Federal privacy laws and policies including but not limited to the E-Government
20     Act of 2002, the Privacy Act of 1974, and OMB Memorandum M-03-22, OMB Guidance for
21     Implementing the Privacy Provisions of the E-Government Act of 2002, as applicable.

38             credential.

 4
 5     a.   The role holder actions defined in this section are to be completed in the following
 6          authoritative systems for USDA.
 7
 8          (1) Authoritative HR System – EmpowHR. Employment status changes made in the
 9              authoritative HR System are automatically fed into the Authoritative PIV Issuance
10              System, Enterprise Physical Access Control System, and other IT systems for the
11              automatic disabling of credentials, physical and logical access during suspension
12              and termination actions. Processing for Federal and non-Federal employees must
13              be completed in the following modules:
14
15                (a) PAR Processing – for Federal employee personnel
16
17                (b) Non-Employee Processing (commonly referred to as Person Model) – for
18                    non-Federal employee personnel and pre-hire selectees for federal employment
19
20

 1        (2) Authoritative PIV Issuance System – USAccess. GSA’s shared service system for
 2            the issuance of the PIV and PIV-I card utilized by USDA.

 4   b.   Mission Areas, Agencies, and Staff Offices
 5
 6        The HSPD-12 program is governed by the USDA HSPD-12 Program Management
 7        Office (PMO). The PMO partners with Mission Area, agency, and staff offices in
 8        implementing HSPD-12 throughout USDA. HSPD-12 implementation is coordinated
 9        through a partnership between the PMO and the designated Mission Area, agency, or
10        staff office Lead and Alternate.
11
12        The PIV process contains critical roles associated with the identity proofing,
13        registration, and issuance process. These roles may be collateral duties assigned to
14        personnel who have other primary duties. The PIV identity proofing, registration and
15        issuance process must adhere to the principle of separation of duties to ensure that no
16        single individual has the capability to issue a PIV credential without the cooperation of
17        another authorized person.
18
19        The following roles must be employed for identity proofing, registration, and issuance.
20
21   c.   Applicant
22
23        The Applicant is an individual who requires a credential as a condition of employment
24        with a Mission Area, agency, or staff office that will be entered into the authoritative
25        PIV issuance system. Applicants will:
26
27        (1) Provide HR User and Sponsor with any necessary information;
28
29        (2) Provide Personnel Security with necessary information required to initiate or
30            validate investigative checks;
31
32        (3) Submit fingerprints for a background check;
33
34        (4) Schedule an enrollment appointment at a USAccess credentialing station;
35
36        (5) Appear for the enrollment appointment at the time and place scheduled;
37
38        (6) Provide the Registrar with two U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services (USCIS)
39            Form I-9 listed identity documents;
40
41        (7) Submit to a digital photo taken by the Registrar;
42
43        (8) Submit ten digital fingerprints (rolls and slaps). If the capture of 10 fingerprints is
44            not feasible due to missing or damaged fingers, this will be annotated in the system;
45
46        (9) Digitally sign the enrollment package;

                                                    8
 1
 2        (10) Activate the credential via biometric verification and Personal Identification
 3             Number (PIN) creation;
 4
 5        (11) Ensure the credential is registered for logical and physical access;
 6
 7        (12) Ensure that the Sponsor or Agency Security Officer is notified if the card is lost or
 8             stolen immediately;
 9
10        (13) Store credentials in a badge holder and fastened to either an item of clothing or
11             lanyard;
12
13        (14) Wear credentials above the waist in such a manner that the ID photograph is clearly
14             visible from the front at all times;
15
16        (15) Wear an armband holder or place the credential in a pocket, if it is permissible, if
17             the applicant works in a hazardous environment where it would be unsafe to wear
18             the credential in the manner outlined; and
19
20        (16) Properly wear and display the credential once the person leaves the hazardous work
21             area (e.g., if the credential had been placed in the pocket, it must be properly worn
22             and displayed again). Mission Area, agency, or staff offices must define the work
23             areas in which it would be unsafe to wear the credential as outlined in this section.
24
25        (17) Return expired or terminated credentials to the Security Officer, local badging
26             office, or mail to the address printed on the back of the PIV card.
27
28        (18) Never share their credential with another person
29
30        (19) Never use their credential to provide physical or logical access to someone other
31             than themselves
32
33        (20) Never leave credential unattended in a workstation or in an unsecured location not
34             on the person when not in use
35
36   d.   HR User
37
38        The HR User is responsible for creating and modifying an Applicant’s information in
39        the authoritative HR system(s). Often the HR User and the Sponsor roles are held by the
40        same person, but it is not required.
41
42   e.   Sponsor
43
44        The Sponsor is the Mission Area, agency, or staff office official who has undergone
45        Sponsor training and is designated to perform Sponsor functions. The Sponsor is
46        responsible for authorizing an individual to apply for a credential. In the case of non-
47        Federal employees, the Sponsor should be the Contracting Officer’s Representative
                                                     9
 1   (COR) or other designated program official as specified in the USDA Contracting Desk
 2   Book. To leverage existing roles in the LincPass process, MobileLinc responsibilities
 3   have been added to the Sponsor role.
 4
 5   Sponsors will:
 6
 7   (1) Validate that the Applicant’s information has been entered into the authoritative HR
 8       system;
 9
10   (2) Ensure that applicant records for non-federal employees are linked to a specific
11       contract number or unique agreement ID in the authoritative HR system. Creating a
12       singular record as a catch all for multiple contracts/grants/agreements is prohibited.
13
14   (3) Ensure that the contracts, grants and agreements in the authoritative HR system are
15       created with valid Period of Performance dates specified in the linked document;
16
17   (4) Ensure that contracts entered in the authoritative HR system are linked to a specific
18       company/organization or sufficient detail is provided in the contract description or
19       notes field;
20
21   (5) Determine the appropriate credential type by utilizing the information in Section 6 –
22       Credential Type Determination;
23
24       (a) Coordinate with agency Personnel Security to determine highest achievable
25           investigative standards, investigation completion status, and other required
26           compliance steps as required in DM 4620-002 Section 6(2)a prior to entering
27           Sponsorship for non-U.S. nationals;
28
29   (6) Modify the Applicant’s identity record in the authoritative database based on
30       updates to user status and relevant information;
31
32   (7) Update and maintain the Applicant’s Sponsorship in the authoritative HR system;
33
34   (8) Maintain current employment status:
35
36       (a) Termination: As needed, set employment status to Terminated in the Human
37           Resources (HR) record in the authoritative HR system; and
38
39       (b) Suspended: As needed, set the employment status to Suspended in the HR
40           record in the authoritative HR system.
41
42       To ensure timely provisioning and de-provisioning of accounts (e.g., desktops,
43       eAuthentication, building access, National Finance Center (NFC) applications,
44       MobileLinc credential management accounts), Mission Area, agency, or staff
45       offices must record hiring and termination events in the authoritative HR database
46       as soon as possible.

30           card will automatically switch from suspended to terminated unless a system or
31           manual action is taken to change the status to Active.
32
33       (c) If card Suspense/Termination is due to notification of imminent risk,

36           subsequently making the change in the authoritative HR system.

10   f.   PIV Registrar
11
12        The Registrar is an individual responsible for identity proofing the Applicant, as well as
13        capturing biographic information, digital photo, and biometrics. Registrars will:
14
15        (1) Manage the schedule for enrollment workstations through the utilization of the
16            online scheduling tool or by providing a direct phone number or email for
17            applicants to make appointments; this includes cancelling appointments in a timely
18            manner and providing direct reach out to applicants if same day cancellation is
19            required.
20
21        (2) Answer any privacy or system related questions that an Applicant may have or refer
22            them to the USDA HSPD-12 Helpdesk;
23
24        (3) Locate and open the Applicant’s information and verify the information with the
25            Applicant;
26
27        (4) Refer the applicant to their Sponsor if applicant’s record cannot be found in the
28            system to investigate and resolve the problem;
29
30        (5) Verify and scan the Applicant’s two identity source (I-9) documents;
31
32        (6) Verify and enter biometric information;
33
34        (7) Capture the Applicant’s facial image in the system via a digital photograph;

 7   g.   HSPD-12 Adjudicator
 8
 9        The HSPD-12 Adjudicator is a Federal employee of the sponsoring Mission Area,
10        agency, and staff office who records the results of the Federal Bureau of Investigation
11        (FBI) National Criminal History Check (FBI fingerprint check) and/or the requisite BI
12        (T1 or higher) into the authoritative HR system. The HSPD-12 Adjudicator role
13        referenced here is not to be confused with the suitability/fitness Adjudicator role as
14        defined by OHS-PDSD. While both roles can be held by the same person, it is not
15        required.
16
17        Final PIV adjudication is aligned with the OHS-PDSD personnel vetting
18        (suitability/fitness) processes. Results of personnel vetting (suitability/fitness) process
19        as determined by OHS-PDSD will be utilized as the final determination for PIV
20        adjudication results. These results will be recorded in the web-based Security Entry
21        Tracking System (webSETS), the system of record for background investigation
22        determinations at USDA, or its successor.
23
24        HSPD-12 Adjudicators will:
25
26        (1) Enter fingerprint check results in the authoritative HR system. Entry of fingerprint
27            check results separately from BI results will expedite printing of the LincPass and is
28            highly recommended for U.S. Nationals. This will result in an interim PIV issuance,
29            pending completion of the BI. If the results are favorable, and all other issuance
30            rules are met this will allow the LincPass to print prior to the completion of the BI.
31            If the results are unfavorable, this will terminate the card issuance process.
32
33            The interim determination process is generally not permitted for non-U.S. Nationals
34            except for exempted categories of FNs considered US Persons in US locations that
35            meet the residency standards for a BI.
36
37            To issue an interim PIV issuance the following steps must be complete:
38
39            (a) Presentation by the appointee or employee of two identity source documents, at
40                least one of which is a Federal or State government-issued picture
41                identification.
42
43            (b) Favorable review of the intended PIV recipient’s completed investigative
44                questionnaire;
45



                                                     13
 1            (c) Initiation of the intended PIV recipient’s required BI (T1 or higher) request,
 2                meaning the request has been submitted to the Federal background
 3                investigation service provider (ISP), and the ISP has scheduled the
 4                investigation; and
 5
 6            (d) Favorable review of the results of the intended PIV recipient’s Federal Bureau
 7                of Investigation (FBI) National Criminal History Check (FBI fingerprint
 8                check) portion of the required BI.
 9
10        (2) Confirm and enter the BI results in the authoritative HR system: entry of BI results

12            this will allow the Applicant to maintain their LincPass. If the results are

 4   b.   PIV Issuance Guidelines
 5
 6        (1) PIV Applicability
 7
 8            (a) PIV requires the implementation of registration, identity proofing, and issuance
 9                procedures in line with the requirements of FIPS PUB 201-3.
10
11                 All individuals must follow the procedures outlined to apply for and receive
12                 their credentials. USDA uses the USAccess program, a certified NIST Special
13                 Publication (SP) 800-79-2, Guidelines for the Authorization of Personal
14                 Identity Verification Card Issuers (PCI) and Derived PIV Credential Issuers
15                 (DPCI), PIV Card Issuing Organization, for PIV issuance.
16
17            (b) A Social Security Number (SSN) is required for entry of applicant records into
18                the authoritative HR systems. SSNs for non-citizens can be obtained through
19                the Social Security Administration process outlined in Publication No. 05-
20                10096. In the event the applicant is a foreign national locally employed by
21                USDA and cannot obtain a SSN a Foreign ID must be obtained through the
22                Department of State.
23
24        (2) Credentialing Standards
25
26            In accordance with OPM guidelines, a PIV credential will not be issued to an
27            individual if any of the following applies:
28
29            (a) The individual is known to be or reasonably suspected of being a terrorist;
30
31            (b) The employer is unable to verify the individual’s claimed identity;
32
33            (c) The employer has a reasonable basis to believe the individual has submitted
34                fraudulent information concerning his or her identity;
35
36            (d) The employer has a reasonable basis to believe the individual will attempt to
37                gain unauthorized access to classified documents, information protected by the
38                Privacy Act, information that is proprietary in nature, or other sensitive or
39                protected information;
40
41            (e) The employer has a reasonable basis to believe the individual will use an
42                identity credential outside the workplace or inappropriately; or
43
44            (f) The employer has a reasonable basis to believe the individual will use federally
45                controlled information systems unlawfully, make unauthorized modifications



                                                  25
 1           to such systems, corrupt or destroy such systems, or engage in inappropriate
 2           uses of such systems.
 3
 4   (3) Revocation and Suspense of PIV Eligibility
 5
 6       (a) Mission Area, agency, and staff offices must develop procedures which adhere
 7           to standards defined by OHS-PDSD to notify the cardholder’s HSPD-12 role
 8           holders (Sponsor, HSPD-12 Adjudicator, and Agency Security Officer) in the
 9           event of any incident or adverse credible information that would impact PIV
10           eligibility.
11
12       (b) HSPD-12 role holders must follow the steps defined in the role holder’s
13           responsibilities section of this document to ensure timely
14           suspension/revocation of the PIV card due to the result of a change in
15           eligibility.
16
17       (c) Making the required status and eligibility changes in the authoritative USDA
18           systems, will automatically remove logical access to IT systems as well as
19           physical access to Enterprise Physical Access Control Systems.
20
21       (d) USDA Mission Area, agency or staff offices may perform Barring Actions at
22           their respective locations outside the National Capitol Region. These actions
23           will be done in accordance with USDA Mission Area, agency or staff office
24           policies and will be reported to OSSP.

 4     USDA participates in the GSA Managed Service Office (MSO) USAccess program for
 5     issuance of the LincPass and AltLinc credentials. With the USAccess Program, the MSO

19     a.   Physical Access Control Systems (PACS)
20
21          (1) This summary outlines the strategic transition of the Electronic Physical Access
22              Control System (ePACS) to meet the mandates of HSPD-12 and achieve FICAM
23              (Federal Identity, Credential, and Access Management) compliance. The objective
24              is to modernize the organization’s physical security infrastructure by ensuring that
25              all facility access points utilize secure, interoperable, and identity-validated
26              credentials (PIV/CAC) in alignment with federal standards.
27
28              (a) Core Mandates
29
30                   1    HSPD-12 (Homeland Security Presidential Directive 12): Requires a
31                        common identification standard for federal employees and contractors.
32                        This is realized through the PIV (Personal Identity Verification) card,
33                        which provides a secure, reliable form of identification.
34
35                   2    FICAM Compliance: The roadmap for implementing HSPD-12. It ensures
36                        that the ePACS can not only read a card but also validate its authenticity
37                        against the issuing authority’s infrastructure in real-time.
38
39              (b) Key Components of a FICAM-Compliant ePACS
40
41                   1    To achieve compliance, the ePACS must integrate three primary
42                        technological layers:
43
44                        a Identity Management (IDMS): Managing the lifecycle of the identity
45                          (onboarding, vetting, and card issuance).
46

2                       b Validation System: A backend service that performs a Certificate
3                         Revocation List (CRL) or Online Certificate Status Protocol (OCSP)
4                         check to ensure the card has not been revoked or expired.
5                       c Physical Access Control (PACS): The hardware (readers, panels) and
6                         software that grant or deny entry based on the validation results.

 6   Appendix B provides the definitions for terms and terminology used throughout this document.
 7   The following definitions are derived from relevant policies and guidance.
 8
 9   Access control. The process of granting or denying requests to access physical facilities or areas,
10   or to logical systems (e.g., computer networks or software applications). See also “logical access
11   control system” and “physical access control system.”
12
13   Access (Routine). Entry into and use of a facility and/or information system on a regular basis
14   without an escort and/or continuous monitoring by a USDA official. The Mission Area, agency,
15   and staff office determination should be based upon the support to successfully complete USDA’s
16   mission functions. Routine access requires a mandatory PIV or PIV-I credential to be issued.
17
18   Access (Unaccompanied). Entry into and use of a facility and/or information system without an
19   escort and/or continuous monitoring by a USDA official. The Mission Area, agency, and staff
20   office’s determination for unaccompanied access should be based upon the support to
21   successfully complete USDA’s mission functions. Unaccompanied access requires a mandatory
22   LincPass to be issued.
23
24   AD-1197 Form. Request for USDA Identification (ID) Badge form.
25
26   AltLinc. USDA has named their common identity PIV-I card the AltLinc, as it is designed to link
27   a person’s identity to an identification card and the card to a person’s ability to access Federal
28   buildings and computer systems. This card will be used to service short-term personnel (less than
29   6 months) and personnel who fail to meet the HSPD-12 minimum investigative requirements. The
30   spelling of the AltLinc PIV-I card is derived from the word alternative as it is meant to serve as an
31   alternative to the LincPass.
32
33   Authorization. The official management decision given by a senior organizational official to
34   authorize operation of an information system and to explicitly accept the risk to the organizational
35   operations (including mission, functions, image, or reputation), organizational assets, individuals,
36   other organizations, and the nation, based on the implementation of an agreed-upon set of security
37   controls. (Source: NIST, Glossary, Retrieved May 3, 2021)
38
39   Barring. Prevention of entry onto federal property.
40
41   Biometric. A measurable physical characteristic used to recognize the identity of an individual.
42   Examples include fingerprints, and facial images. A biometric system uses biometric data for
43   authentication purposes.

45   Contractor. An individual under contract to USDA (for the purpose of HSPD-12
46   implementation).

 3                         EXAMPLES OF CREDENTIAL TOPOLOGIES
 4
 5
 6   1. FRONT REQUIREMENTS OF PIV CREDENTIAL
 7
 8     a.   Mandatory Items on the Front of the PIV Card
 9
10          (1) Zone 1F – Photograph. The photograph must be placed in the upper left corner and
11              be a full-frontal pose from top of the head to shoulder. A minimum of 300 dots per
12              inch (dpi) resolution must be used. The background should follow
13              recommendations set forth in SP 800-76-2, Biometric Specifications for Personal
14              Identity Verification.
15
16          (2) Zone 2F – Name. The full name must be printed directly under the photograph in
17              capital letters. The full name must be composed of a Primary Identifier (i.e.,
18              surnames or family names) and a Secondary Identifier (i.e., pre-names or given
19              names). The printed name must match the name on the identity source documents
20              provided during identity proofing and registration to the extent possible. The full
21              name must be printed in the <Primary Identifier>, <Secondary Identifier>, format.
22              The entire full name should be printed on available lines of Zone 2F and either
23              identifier could be wrapped. The wrapped identifier must be indicated with “>”
24              character at the end of the line. The identifiers may be printed on separate lines if
25              each fits on one line. Departments and agencies must use the largest font size of 7
26              to 10-points that allows the full name to be printed. The font size 7-point allows
27              space for 3 lines and must only be used if the full name does not fit on two lines
28              with font size 8-point.
29
30          (3) Zone 8F – Employee Affiliation. An employee affiliation must be printed on the
31              card. Some examples of employee affiliation are “Employee,” “Contractor,”
32              “Active Duty,” and “Civilian.”
33
34          (4) Zone 10F – Agency, Department, or Organization. The organizational affiliation
35              must be printed as depicted in Figure 3, Front of PIV Credential.
36
37          (5) Zone 14F – Card Expiration Date. The card expiration date must be printed on the
38              card. The card expiration date must be in a YYYYMMMDD format whereby the
39              MMM characters represent the three-letter month abbreviation as follows: JAN,
40              FEB, MAR, APR, MAY, JUN, JUL, AUG, SEP, OCT, NOV, and DEC. The Zone
41              14F expiration date must be printed in Arial 6 to 9-point bold.

21   BACKGROUND: Executive Order 11935, Citizenship Requirements for Federal
22   Employment (September 2, 1976) restricts appointing non-citizens in competitive service
23   positions covered by Title 5, United States Code (U.S.C.). Since the passage of the
24   Immigration Reform and Control Act of 1986, employers have been responsible for ensuring
25   that the people they hire are eligible to work in the United States (U.S.). The Immigration
26   and Nationality Act of 1990 added other requirements, one of which is that employers may
27   not discriminate against employees by requesting more or different documents than required.
28   The Immigration and Nationality Act, as modified, provides at 8 U.S.C. 1324(a), that it is
29   unlawful for a person or other entity to employ an undocumented noncitizen. In 1996, Public
30   Law (P.L.) 104-208 stated that the term “entity” includes an entry in any branch of the
31   Federal Government. Regulations that implement immigration laws are published in Title 8,
32   Code of Federal Regulations (CFR).
33
34   PURPOSE: The purpose of this memorandum is to implement 5 CFR 7.3, §213.3102(bb),
35   and §338.101, in conjunction with the Immigration Reform and Control Act of 1986,
36   Immigration and Nationality Act of 1990, P.L. 104-208, 8 U.S.C. 13249(a), 8, CFR 274(a),
37   the Consolidated Appropriations Act (Fiscal Year 2022, Division E, Section 704) ban on
38   paying certain non-citizen, general statutory restrictions that may be applicable to Mission
39   Areas, Agencies and Staff Offices, and general statutory restrictions on the expenditure of
40   funds.
41
42   This memorandum applies to all competitive service Federal positions and Federal contractor
43   positions within the U.S. Department of Agriculture in the continental United States. This
44   memorandum does not apply to non-U.S. citizens who are:
45   a. A citizen of another country and the U.S. at the same time (dual U.S. citizenship or dual
46   U.S. nationality).

 2   b. Hired for excepted service or Senior Executive Service Federal appointments in the
 3   continental U.S., as permitted by immigration law and any appropriations act ban.

 6   accordance with immigration laws.

20   POLICY:
21   It is the policy of the Federal Government to employ only U.S. citizens or persons owing
22   permanent allegiance to the U.S. under 5 CFR 213.3102(bb)1a non-citizen may be hired in
23   the absence of a qualified citizen when authorized by the Office of Personnel Management
24   (OPM). §7.3(c) grants OPM the authority, as an exception to this rule and to the extent
25   permitted by law, to authorize the appointment of noncitizens to positions in the competitive
26   service when necessary to promote the efficiency of the service in specific cases or for
27   temporary appointments. Non-citizens may only be employed in positions that do not involve
28   significant authority and responsibility in connection with the management of the
29   Department. The term “management” includes, but is not limited to, planning, programming,
30   policy formulation, direction, supervision of operations, and control.
31
32   A non-citizen hired in the absence of a qualified citizen may only be given a Schedule A
33   excepted appointment (5 CFR 213.3102(bb) and must be approved by OPM. The position
34   must be withdrawn from the competitive service for the period it is filled by the non-citizen.
35   T non-citizen hire will not acquire competitive status and may not be promoted or reassigned
36   to another position in the competitive service, except in situations where a qualified citizen is
37   not available.
38
39   Non-citizens may only be employed through Schedule A if all of the requirements of general
40   statutory restrictions on the expenditure of funds, special statutory restrictions applicable to
41   USDA, Immigration law, and the Appropriations Act ban on pay are met. Additionally, only
42   OPM is authorized to approve exceptions to this rule and appoint noncitizens in the
43   competitive service when the hire is a temporary appointment, or the position ensures the
44   efficiency of the service (Title 5 CFR § 7.3).

 6   the attainment of at least a bachelor’s degree or its equivalent. The intent of the H-1B
 7   provisions is to help employers who cannot otherwise obtain needed business skills and
 8   abilities from the US workers by authorizing the temporary employment of qualified
 9   individuals who are not otherwise authorized to work in the US. USDA Mission Areas,
10   Agencies and Staff Offices are not prohibited awarding contracts to companies that hire
11   immigrants on H-1B visas.

11
12   BACKGROUND
13   On December 15, 2020, the Office of Personnel Management (OPM), in its capacity
14   as the Credentialing Executive Agent and Suitability Executive Agent, issued a
15   memorandum titled, Credentialing Standards Procedures for Issuing Personnel
16   Identity Verification Cards under HSPD-12 and New Requirements for Suspension
17   or Revocation of Eligibility for Personal Identity Verification Credentials
18   (Credentialing Standards).
19
20   The memorandum guidance covers all individuals including any person who
21   performs, or who seeks to perform, work for or on behalf of the executive branch,
22   such as Federal employees and contractors, or those who interact with the executive
23   branch, such as guest researchers, volunteers, and seasonal employees, in a way that
24   requires vetting.
25
26   The intent of the memorandum was to provide greater detail and clarity on several
27   areas, to include existing policy on the investigation requirements and credentialing
28   process for non-United States nationals (foreign nationals). Due to limitations in an
29   Investigative Service Provider’s (ISP) ability to collect background investigation
30   information in locations outside the United States, alternative investigative action is
31   needed for a foreign national.
32
33   INVESTIGATION REQUIREMENTS
34   Prior to releasing a background investigation on a foreign national, Personnel
35   Security offices must review the “Where You Have Lived” section on the Standard
36   Form 85, Questionnaire for Non-Sensitive Positions.
37    A Tier 1 investigation can be requested on a foreign national who has been
38   in the United States or a United States Territory for 3 or more years,
39   continuously and immediately preceding the start of Federal affiliation.
40    A Tier 1 investigation cannot be requested on a foreign national who has
41   been in the United States or a United States Territory for less than 3 years
42   preceding the start of Federal affiliation.
43
44   For foreign nationals who do not meet the above residency requirement for a Tier 1,
45   Personnel Security offices must alternatively request a Special Agreement Check
46   (SAC – OFI 86C, updated June 2022), case type 92, with our ISP, the Defense

                                                   G-1
 1   Counterintelligence and Security Agency (DCSA), coded accordingly to obtain the
 2   following four checks:
 3   a. FBI fingerprint based National Criminal History Check (NCHC) – code
 4   B0 (numerical zero); must obtain a classifiable result. Resubmit new
 5   fingerprint chart if initial result is returned as unclassifiable.
 6   b. FBI Investigations Files (FBIF; Name Check) – code C0 (numerical zero)
 7   c. Name check against the Terrorist Screening Database (TSDB) – satisfied
 8   through the above classifiable FBI fingerprint check which includes a
 9   check of the Known and Suspected Terrorist File.
10   d. US Citizenship and Immigration Services (USCIS) check against the
11   Systematic Alien Verification for Entitlements (SAVE) program – code I0
12   (numerical zero)
13
14   This alternative SAC option provided by DCSA is supported by PDSD and is
15   effective immediately. PDSD will include this language in future Departmental
16   Regulation (DR) updates.
17
18   The requesting agency is required to maintain the signed release for the individual
19   from the SF85 to support the SAC. Personnel Security offices will document these
20   checks as SACs (Inv Type field) on the Entry on Duty screen in the web-based
21   Security Entry Tracking System (webSETS).
22
23   Foreign nationals who have a favorably adjudicated SAC will be issued an
24   alternative identity credential until the individual has resided for 3 years in the
25   United States or United States Territory, at which time a Tier 1 investigation can be
26   requested.
27
28   In addition to the SAC submission, and to further support the overall risk
29   determination on a foreign national, agencies are reminded to submit foreign
30   national vetting (FNV) checks to the Office of Homeland Security (OHS) in
31   accordance with DR 4600-004, Foreign National Visits and Assignments Vetting,
32   which requires additional screening on a foreign national prior to their arrival or
33   onboarding of a foreign national at a USDA facility, or working remotely, in the
34   United States.
