1. PURPOSE

     a.   This Departmental manual (DM) establishes procedures for the United States
          Department of Agriculture’s (USDA) enterprise information technology governance
          (EITG) framework and accompanying governance processes. USDA’s EITG
          framework is called the Integrated Information Technology Governance Framework
          (IITGF).

     b.   This DM provides an overview and implementation guidance for the USDA IITGF. The
          Integrated Information Technology Governance Framework: Guidebook identifies the
          activities of each of the eight decision gates of the IITGF. Decision gates are aligned
          with an investment’s lifecycle phase and enable USDA to manage its EITG processes.

          (1) This DM also defines the alignment activities and responsibilities of the
              Information Technology (IT) Executive Governance Board. These measures
              facilitate effective IT decision-making within USDA and include touchpoints with
              other governance, management, and control processes. These include USDA’s IT
              Portfolio Management (ITPM) division, USDA’s System Development Lifecycle
           approach, project management, enterprise architecture (EA), the risk management
           framework, IT security and privacy controls, and Departmental Regulation (DR)
           3575-004, Information Technology Security Baselines and Security Control
           Tailoring. Implementing an Integrated IT Governance Framework further supports
           the USDA mission by providing organizational alignment for decisions related to
           technology components, services, and applications.


2. SCOPE

  a.   This DM applies to all Mission Areas, agencies, and staff offices with USDA major and
       non-major IT investments.

  b.   The USDA IITGF applies to all new and existing USDA major IT investments and
       projects. This includes enhancements to existing major investments and projects
       associated with operations, maintenance, and disposition.

  c.   The USDA IITGF also applies and should be leveraged during governance for any non-
       major investments at a Mission Area, agency, or staff office.

4. BACKGROUND

  a.   There are various legislative and regulatory drivers for implementing ITPM. Many legal
       reforms emphasize the need for Federal agencies to significantly improve how they plan,
       select, fund, control, and evaluate IT initiatives. The Federal approach to ITPM has
       been the select-control-evaluate model championed by OMB in OMB Circular A-11,
       Preparation, Submission, and Execution of the Budget; the Government Accountability
       Office (GAO); and industry experts since the early 1990s.

  b.   To give Mission Areas, agencies, and staff offices guidance on implementing CCA and
       FITARA, OMB offers Circular A-130, Managing Information as a Strategic Resource.

  c.   The Consolidated and Further Continuing Appropriations Act, 2015, P.L. 113-235,
       Division A, Title VII, Section 706 prohibits the use of funds to acquire new IT systems
       or significant upgrades without certain approval and concurrences. These include the
       approval of the Chief Information Officer (CIO) and the concurrence of the Executive IT
       Investment Review Board (E-Board) and the Committees on Appropriations of both
       Houses of Congress.


5. PROCEDURES

  a.   Definition of IT Governance

       IT governance involves collaborating to make strategic decisions about technology
       within an organization. It connects business and IT leaders, ensuring alignment and
       adaptability in the ever-evolving IT landscape. It covers four critical areas: strategic
       alignment, value delivery, risk management, and resource management.

  b.   Summary of the IITGF

       (1) The IITGF is a cohesive, streamlined governance structure that provides for
           executive-level decision-making. It outlines the primary role that each governance
           board plays within the EITG process. This ensures the accountability and success
           of governance objectives.

       (2) In the IITGF, governance boards and members represent the full complement of and
           formal authority over the business, financial, and IT organizations of USDA.

       (3) The IITGF is a comprehensive framework of policies and processes that ensures
           USDA’s formal, responsive, and effective oversight of IT resources. It integrates
           enterprise and mission area governance, standardizes intake and review workflows,
           and aligns IT decision-making with USDA’s strategic priorities, compliance
           mandates, and operational needs. This ensures operational efficiency and value
           delivery in the implementation of IT by enabling timely, evidence-based decisions;
           reducing duplication and rework through standardized artifacts; and ensuring that



                                                3
         resources are directed toward initiatives with clear institutional fit, measurable
         impact, and sustained alignment with USDA’s mission. The framework spans the
         entire IT lifecycle from initiation through disposition. The Integrated Information
         Technology Governance Framework: Guidebook defines a series of eight
         governance decision gates and associated investment lifecycle phases. These
         promote effective executive oversight and management of IT investments.

     (4) An investment tailoring agreement (ITA) formalizes the requirements for requests
         to the Integrated Advisory Board (IAB) for customizing or waiving IITGF-specific
         phases, activities, decision gates, deliverables, or investment governance reviews.
         These requests are based on specific investment requirements or business needs and
         can occur in any of the eight decision gates of the IITGF.

          (a) Personnel can use an ITA to request and obtain authorization. They can also
              use an ITA to record the rationale for using, omitting, or integrating specific
              IITGF decision gate reviews. An ITA determines the selection and granularity
              of deliverables for the investment or project. It indicates the parameters of
              scope, schedule, budget, and quality, including any exceptions.

          (b) The Investment Manager or Project Manager (PM) for the investment must
              initiate and endorse the ITA. After approval by a USDA IT governance board
              or its delegate, the ITA indicates permission for the project to advance.

c.   The Eight Decision Gates of the IITGF

     (1) Procedure for the Eight Decision Gates

         The procedure for each of the eight decision gates is the same, except for minor
         differences noted in the description of each decision gate. Each investment or
         project is required to go through the following steps for each of the eight decision
         gates to meet IITGF requirements and obtain the necessary reviews and approvals
         from the required enterprise governance boards.

         (a) The Mission Area, Agency, or Staff Office Integrated Project Team (IPT):

             1   Collaboratively identifies, addresses, and resolves issues (determines
                 requirements);

             2   Establishes project baselines and contract requirements; and

             3   Provides required information to the Investment Manager or PM for
                 developing the governance documentation package based on the decision
                 gate.

         (b) The Investment Manager or PM:




                                             4
   1   Reviews the completed decision gate approval documentation package; and

   2   Submits the approved documentation package to the Mission Area Agency,
       or Staff Office Investment Review Board (IRB).

(c) The Mission Area, Agency, or Staff Office IRB:

   1   Conducts an evaluation of the submitted materials; and

   2   Decides whether to approve the governance request.

        a    If the governance request is approved, the IRB provides the required
             updates, action items, and conditions (if applicable) to the Investment
             Manager or PM.

        b    If the governance request is not approved, the IRB notifies the
             Investment Manager or PM of the rejection and reason.

(d) The Investment Manager or PM:

   1   If the governance request was approved, the Investment Manager or PM
       sends the Decision gate documentation package to the Enterprise
       Governance Team Liaison prior to the deadline for their IAB.

   2   If the governance request is not approved, the Investment Manager or PM
       updates the Decision gate documentation package and resubmits it to the
       Mission Area, Agency, or Staff Office IRB.

(e) The Enterprise Governance Team Liaison:

   1   Reviews the decision gate documentation package, supporting
       documentation, and IRB assessment for completion and accuracy; and

   2   Notifies the E-Board and IAB of upcoming activities for this decision gate
       and routes materials and inputs to the E-Board and IAB for review and
       distribution to IAB and E-Board members in preparation for the meetings.

(f) The E-Board and IAB:

   1   Review the documentation submitted for the governance request; and

   2   Decide whether to approve or disapprove it and provide decision memos
       signed by the appropriate board chair.

        a    If the E-Board and IAB approve the decision gate documentation
             package, they send a signed approval memo, with any applicable

                  (POC).

             b    If the E-Board and IAB do not approve the decision gate

(2) Descriptions of the Decision Gates with Procedure Exceptions

    (a) Decision Gate 1: Initiation Review

        1   Decision gate 1, personnel assess the need for a proposed investment. They
            evaluate the alignment with Departmental, Mission Area, agency, or staff
            office strategic planning efforts. This decision gate allows Mission Areas,
            agencies, and staff offices to develop the investment concept with
            preliminary business requirements, performance measures, benefits, and
            costs. It allows the integrated IT governance boards to review these
            proposals and assess the impact and benefit to the overall IT portfolio.

        2   Each newly proposed IT project or initiative proceeds through IITGF
            decision gate 1. The IAB decides whether it is a major or non-major
            investment. DR 3130-008, Definition of Major Information Technology
            Investments, defines major investments. DR 3130-009, Non-Major
            Information Technology (IT) Investments, defines non-major investments.

             a    If the new proposed IT project or initiative is a major IT investment
                  or within a major investment it will follow the governance path in the
                  Integrated Information Technology Governance Framework:
                  Guidebook. Review, recommendation, and decision authority at each
                  level will vary based on the criteria and decision type per DR 3130-
                  008.

             b    If it is a non-major investment, the Office of the Chief Information
                  Officer (OCIO) Information Resource Management Center (IRMC)
                  IT Strategic Planning and Governance (ITSPG) division will inform
                  the Investment Manager or PM that the governance delegation will
                  return to the Mission Area, agency, or staff office. It will then
                  proceed under that Mission Area, agency, or staff office governance
                  structure.

    (b) Decision Gate 2: Investment Review

        1   Decision gate 2 ensures that the proposed or existing investment best
            supports the Department’s mission and objectives. The investments are




                                       6
       prioritized and evaluated for performance as measured by cost, schedule,
       performance, and risk, as well as technical alignment.

(c) Decision Gate 3: Requirements Review

   1   Decision gate 3 is required to determine the effectiveness, completion, and
       accuracy levels achieved throughout the investment’s planning activities.
       This decision gate provides the integrated IT governance boards with
       information to further understand the strategy by which the proposed
       investment will achieve its scope, cost, and schedule objectives.

(d) Decision Gate 4: Design Review

   1   Decision gate 4 is for reviewing an investment’s functional requirements.
       The functional requirements must be complete, unambiguous, and
       actionable enough to support detailed system design, acquisition planning,
       and downstream implementation. This decision gate also allows the
       integrated IT governance boards to gain a complete understanding of the
       requirements and ensure that the requirements can support a detailed design
       of the proposed system.

(e) Decision Gate 5: Development and Test Review

   1   Decision gate 5 ensures that any systems under review can proceed into
       system fabrication, development, and testing by conducting a formal design
       review. This review compares the system’s final design against the stated
       performance requirements, cost, schedule, risk, and other constraints. It does
       so by examining product specifications, engineering drawings, and
       configuration item documentation to confirm alignment with the product
       baseline. Independent reviewers and subject matter experts validate that
       each product in the baseline is fully represented in the detailed design
       documentation. This structured evaluation, often supported by checklists,
       compliance matrices, and risk assessments, provides confidence that the
       system is ready to enter testing.

(f) Decision Gate 6: Implementation Review

   1   Decision gate 6 is a review of the implementation, ensuring that the
       development and testing of the investment systems satisfy the desired
       functional requirements. It accomplishes this by requiring evidence from
       unit tests, integration tests, and system-level verification reports that
       demonstrate compliance with business needs, functional requirements, and
       service level agreements. Reviewers also assess user procedures, training
       materials, and operational documentation to confirm they meet stakeholder
       needs. The gate uses test results, defect tracking, and performance metrics to
       verify that components adhere to physical constraints and operate as



                                  7
                 described in the user documentation. This structured review process ensures
                 that the system is not only built correctly but is also usable and supportable
                 in practice.

         (g) Decision Gate 7: Operations and Maintenance (O&M)

             1   Decision gate 7 determines whether system components achieve expected
                 benefits. It provides an evaluation of the investment’s development process.
                 It determines whether the investment is ready to go into O&M mode. From
                 the evaluation, there is a recommendation for continuing or modifying an
                 investment.

         (h) Decision Gate 8: Disposition Review

             1   Decision gate 8 determines whether system components are still providing
                 their benefits at a reasonable cost, have reached the end of life, or will be
                 disposed of (retired or decommissioned). After an evaluation, there is a
                 recommendation for terminating or disposing of, an investment.

d.   USDA’s IT Baseline Management Process

     (1) Earned value management (EVM) is the integrated management system that USDA
         uses to coordinate the work scope, schedule, and cost goals of a program or
         contract. USDA also uses it to objectively measure progress toward these goals.
         Mission Areas, agencies, and staff offices must implement IT baseline management
         processes that meet certain minimum requirements that align with EVM best
         practices and the USDA’s enterprise governance process.

     (2) USDA requires compliance for all investments that meet any one of the following
         criteria:

         (a) All new IT investments for any Mission Area, agency, or staff office are
             planning to spend $20 million or more on development, modernization, and
             enhancement (DME) over the lifecycle; or

         (b) All major IT investments currently in process and budgeted for $20 million or
             more in DME during the remainder of the lifecycle; or

         (c) Any investment for which a Mission Area, agency, or staff office is planning to
             spend $1 million or more in DME in current-year funding (the Mission Area,
             agency, or staff office must use EVM to track investment cost, schedule, and
             performance).

e.   Requirements for Integrated Baseline Reviews




                                             8
     (1) An integrated baseline review is a joint assessment between the USDA Federal PM
         and the contractor to establish a mutual understanding of the performance
         measurement baseline (PMB). This understanding provides for an agreement on a
         plan of action to evaluate the risks inherent in the PMB and the management
         processes that operate during program execution.

     (2) PMs must conduct integrated baseline reviews on all major investments. They must
         conduct these within 180 calendar days from project start.

     (3) The Mission Area, agency, or staff office’s Investment Manager or PM will submit
         an integrated baseline review report during the Select phase of the CPIC process.
         The report will help the Investment Manager or PM understand whether:

         (a) The scope of the PMB is consistent with authorizing documents;

         (b) All activities and deliverables required by the statement of work are in the work
             breakdown structure (WBS);

         (c) Work packages are at the appropriate level of the WBS;

         (d) Management control processes are in place; and

         (e) There are risks in the PMB associated with cost, schedules, and resources, and
             there are corrective actions where necessary.

f.   Requirements for Rebaselines

     (1) A rebaseline is the major realignment of the PMB to improve the correlation
         between the work plan and the baseline budget, scope, and schedule. Rebaselining
         does not add or change the existing scope of work. It merely changes how and
         when personnel may accomplish it within the constraints of the existing contract.

     (2) Personnel must not approve baseline changes for the sole purpose of offsetting
         variances. Baseline changes are, however, acceptable for unexpected growth within
         the currently authorized work scope, rate changes, risk handling, and other program
         unknowns. Rebaselining to offset variances erases important information useful for
         effective project management. This is counter to the purpose of EVM. However, if
         measuring and managing the current baseline is no longer viable, and EVM is no
         longer an effective tool for the management of the project, the project may be
         rebaselined. The criteria for a rebaseline are as follows:

         (a) The schedule variance is equal to or greater than 10 percent; or

         (b) The budget variance is equal to or greater than 10 percent, or equal to or less
             than minus 10 percent; or




                                             9
         (c) There is a scope change (any modification, adjustments, or additions to the
             project’s original goals, deliverables, or requirements).

g.   Requirements for Portfolio Restructures

     (1) Portfolio restructures are an integral aspect of strategic portfolio management
         (SPM). They should be part of an ongoing process that focuses on aligning
         USDA’s IT resources with its strategic business goals.

     (2) As USDA continues to adopt agile development methods to respond to the rapidly
         changing business environment, there is also a need for agility at the strategic level.
         Portfolio restructurings need to be able to quickly align budgets, resources, projects,
         and architecture roadmaps to new business strategies.

     (3) A portfolio restructuring enables an organization to neatly organize the information
         needed to optimize portfolios, improve agility, and set the pace for change.
         Integrating portfolios enables impact analyses that reduce planning errors and
         improve synergies between the portfolios.

     (4) SPM needs to be a continuous process. An organization needs to be able to
         continuously adapt to be resilient. This means continuous analysis, decision-
         making, IT road mapping, planning, execution, monitoring, and finally returning
         full circle to analysis. This needs to happen across all organizational hierarchies
         and units, from strategy to execution. The following items fall under the criteria for
         a portfolio restructure. Personnel should use the portfolio restructure checklist as
         appropriate:

         (a) Addition of new investments;

         (b) Upgrading investments;

         (c) Downgrading investments;

         (d) Eliminating investments;

         (e) Consolidating investments; and

         (f) Splitting investments.

h.   Requirements for Governance Touchpoints (Annual or Incremental)

     (1) The owners of all USDA major investments must brief the IAB at a minimum
         annually to ensure alignment with milestones, procurement, and other strategic
         priorities.




                                             10
     (2) An annual touchpoint should be completed for steady state or O&M projects that
         have not been briefed to the IAB in over 1 year.

     (3) DME projects that have passed Decision gate 2 and are utilizing any agile
         development methodology should have established reoccurring incremental
         touchpoints with the IAB that align with the implementation of any major release.

i.   Preparing for Governance Boards and Facilitating Meetings (IAB and E-Board)

     (1) At a minimum, IAB meetings will be scheduled monthly and E-Board meetings
         scheduled quarterly. The chair of each board can call an ad hoc meeting as
         necessary. Each investment must go through their Mission Area, agency, or staff
         office governance board before briefing the IAB and then the E-Board repository.
         The process should be as follows:

         (a) The Mission Area, agency, or staff office governance board approves
             investment and applicable projects.

         (b) The Mission Area, agency, or staff office capital planning team or Investment
             Manager coordinates with their ITSPG Liaison to select and confirm the IAB
             meeting date.

         (c) The Mission Area, agency, or staff office provides briefing and supporting
             documentation to the ITSPG Liaison and IT governance mailbox at least 3
             weeks before the meeting.

         (d) Briefing, agendas, and supporting documents are distributed to IAB voting
             members no later than within 5 business days before the meeting.

         (e) Voting members have 5 business days to develop questions, comments,
             feedback, and action items or conditions.

         (f) The OCIO IRMC ITSPG Governance Team delivers the internal pre-brief to the
             IAB Chair and Vice Chair 2 days before the meeting.

         (g) Hold the IAB meeting. The Chair or Vice Chair facilitates the meeting.

         (h) IAB meeting minutes are finalized and distributed NLT 5 business days after
             the meeting.

         (i) Send the final draft of the IAB meeting memo with action items and conditions
             for the CIO’s signature NLT 5 business days after the meeting.

         (j) Distribute the CIO-signed IAB memo to all stakeholders NLT 1 business day
             after receipt. Store it in the SharePoint IT governance document repository




                                           11
               along with the briefing, meeting minutes, and other supporting documentation,
               in accordance with DR 3080-001, Records Management.

           (k) Brief each recommended investment to the E-Board. Follow the same steps and
               timeframes for preparing and briefing the E-Board.

      (2) Attendance will be captured at each meeting:

           (a) 75 percent of voting member attendance is necessary for a quorum for
               conducting business and making recommendations regarding actions for items
               coming before the boards.

           (b) Primary participants must attend or designate a proxy at least 24 hours in
               advance of the meeting.

      (3) Meeting objectives can be accomplished using multiple forums:

           (a) In-person meetings;

           (b) Virtual meetings; or

           (c) Documentation review, without a meeting.

      (4) Voting members have the following options and decisions:

           (a) Voting Options: Approve, Approve with Conditions, or Disapprove.

           (b) Decision Options: Identify Action Items and Conditions, Identify Points of
               Contact (POC), and Identify Due Dates.

      (5) A simple majority (51 percent) of those voting in favor of a motion will pass the
          motion. The Chair will only vote in the event of a tie vote among voting members.

      (6) Action items and condition tracking:

           (a) The respective organizations and the Mission Area, agency, or staff office POCs
               will track action items and conditions.

           (b) A member of the Governance Team or ITSPG Liaison will notify all
               stakeholders upon the resolution of the action item.

         (d) The Vice Chair will lead the IAB in any situation when the Chair is not
             available.

     (1) Represent their organization’s interests in proposed decisions, investments, projects,
         policies, and expenditures;

     (2) Communicate and support the decisions at their respective Mission Areas, agencies,

         (a) Attending meetings;

         (b) Reviewing and assessing all submitted IT investment deliverables on the agenda
             to make recommendations, approvals, or rejections as appropriate; and

                                         DEFINITIONS


Control Phase. A phase of the ITPM process. Its purpose is to ensure that, as projects develop
and investment expenditures occur, the investment and its associated projects and activities
continue to meet mission or business needs at the expected levels of cost and risk. The key
objectives are:

   a.   To ensure that corrective actions are taken quickly to address any deficiencies in project
        or operational components; and

   b.   To allow an organization to adjust its objectives for an investment and appropriately
        modify expected outcomes if its mission or business needs change. (Source: CCA)

Earned Value Management (EVM). The technique for measuring project performance and
assessing progress during project execution. This system aims to help people identify trends,
predict problems, consider solutions, and manage performance more effectively. Essentially,
EVM measures project performance against the baselines outlined in a project scope
management plan. Using original goals as benchmarks, people can measure progress more
accurately and draw actionable conclusions. (Sources: OMB A-11, FY 2025 Capital
Programming Guide and subsequent versions)

Enterprise. An organization that includes interdependent resources (people, organizations,
technology) that must be integrated for effective operation.” (Sources: OMB A-11, FY2025
Capital Programming Guide and subsequent versions)

Enterprise IT Governance (EITG). A set of responsibilities and practices exercised by a board
and executive management. EITG provides strategic direction. It ensures that objectives are
achieved. It ascertains that risks are managed appropriately. It verifies that the enterprise’s
resources are used responsibly. (Source: DR 3130-010)

Evaluate Phase. A phase of the ITPM process. Its purpose is to compare actual versus expected
benefits and costs of IT investments and projects. This allows personnel to assess return on
investment, customer satisfaction, and value to an organization in meeting its mission and
business needs. (Source: CCA)

Governance. A set of processes that ensures the effective and efficient use of IT in enabling an
organization to achieve its goals. (Source: DR 3130-010)

Information System. A discrete set of information resources organized for the collection,
processing, maintenance, use, sharing, dissemination, or disposition of information. (Source: 44
U.S.C. § 3502(8), Definitions)




                                               B-1
Information Technology (IT). Any equipment or interconnected system or subsystem of
equipment used in the automatic acquisition, storage, manipulation, management, movement,
control, display, switching, interchange, transmission, or reception of data or information by an
executive agency. Related to the terms capital asset, IT investment, program, project, sub-
project, service, and system. It also includes computers, ancillary equipment (including imaging
peripherals, input, output, and storage devices necessary for security and surveillance),
peripheral equipment designed to be controlled by the central processing unit of a computer,
software, and firmware. It also includes similar procedures, services (including support
services), and related resources. It does not include any equipment acquired by a Federal
contractor incidental to a Federal contract (per CCA). However, OMB policy includes this:
“supercomputers, software for mission systems, telecommunications, and satellite signal
processing.” (Source: USDA DME and O&M Definitions (and Additional IT-Related
Definitions))

Information Technology Portfolio Management (ITPM). The combination and management of
all IT assets, resources, and investments owned or planned by an organization to achieve its
strategic goals, objectives, and mission. (Sources: FY 2022 IT Budget – Capital Planning
Guidance and subsequent versions)

Integrated Information Technology Governance Framework (IITGF). An integral part of EITG.
Consists of the leadership, structures, and processes that ensure that an organization’s IT sustains
and extends its strategies and objectives. Integrated IT governance requires a structure and
processes to support repeatable decision-making, alignment of IT activities to the enterprise’s
strategic goals and objectives, and a clear understanding of authority and accountability.
(Source: Integrated Information Technology Governance Framework: Guidebook)

Integrated Project Team (IPT): Are formed to support the planning, execution, and governance
of IT investments. At a minimum, IPTs should include representation from business/program
leadership, IT, cybersecurity, acquisition, and financial management to ensure cross-functional
coordination and accountability across the investment lifecycle. For offices with limited IT
staffing, enterprise resources such as EPMO liaisons, ITAs, or OCIO support may be designated
to fulfill required roles. IPTs are responsible for developing governance artifacts, preparing for
decision gate reviews, and ensuring traceability of scope, schedule, budget, and quality
parameters. Mission Areas are encouraged to tailor IPT composition based on project
complexity and operational context, while maintaining compliance with IITGF expectations. For
assistance in forming an IPT or clarifying role requirements, Mission Areas should contact
Enterprise Governance points of contact listed in the guidebook or governance portal.

Investment Manager or Project Manager (PM). The Investment Manager or PM is responsible
for ensuring that funding and resources are in place to support an IT investment and that they are
used appropriately. These duties can also be performed by a Program Manager or another
individual in a similar role who has oversight responsibilities of an investment, project, or
system. (Sources: FY 2022 IT Budget – Capital Planning Guidance and subsequent versions)

Investment Tailoring Agreement (ITA). Tailoring is a request to the IAB for customizing or
waiving IITGF-specific phases, activities, decision gates, deliverables, or investment governance



                                                B-2
reviews. It is based on specific investment requirements or specific business needs. Personnel
can complete tailoring requests at any stage of the IITGF process documented in the ITA
deliverable. It is the Mission Area, agency, or staff office PM’s responsibility to ensure that the
major investment meets the required governance needs. (Source: Integrated Information
Technology Governance Framework: Guidebook)

IT Investment Management. A management process to identify, select, control, and evaluate
investments in information systems and resources. The process effectively and efficiently:

   a.   Links mission needs, information, and IT;

   b.   Integrates with organizational planning, budgeting, procurement, management
        (financial, human resources, and program), and assessment;

   c.   Coordinates with the Department’s current and targeted EA; and

   d.   Maximizes the value while assessing and managing the risks in the selection, control,
        and evaluation of information resources.

(Sources: FY 2022 IT Budget – Capital Planning Guidance and subsequent versions)

Lifecycle. A set of processes and their temporal relationships that describe a continuous flow of
actions and states associated with the existence of a system. The linear sequence of phases of a
system’s existence that span an initiating action to a closing action, with an implied future re-
execution of the sequence. (Source: USDA DME and O&M Definitions (and Additional IT-
Related Definitions))

Major IT Investment. An IT investment in Part 1 and Part 2 of the IT portfolio requires special
management attention because of its:

   a.   Importance to the mission or function of the government;

   b.   Significant program or policy implications;

   c.   High executive visibility;

   d.   High development, operating, or maintenance costs;

   e.   Unusual funding mechanism; or

   f.   Definition as “major” by the Agency’s ITPM process.

OMB may work with the Agency to declare IT investments as major IT investments. Agencies
must consult with assigned OMB Desk Officers and Resource Management Offices regarding
which investments are considered “major.” Investments not considered “major” are “non-




                                                B-3
major.” (Sources: FY 2022 IT Budget – Capital Planning Guidance and subsequent versions;
DR 3130-008)

Major IT Release. A major release introduces wide-ranging changes to the existing software
version, including new features to improve the user experience. For example, it might add new
features or functionality. (Source: USDA Definition of Major and Minor Software Releases)

Mission Area Investment Review Board (IRB): Is an essential governance bodies that ensure IT
investments align with USDA strategic priorities, comply with IITGF Gate requirements, and
reflect sound business and technical planning. At minimum, IRBs must be chartered with defined
decision authority, including representatives from program leadership, IT, acquisition, and
finance, and maintain documented procedures for intake, review, and escalation. IRBs should
meet regularly to evaluate proposals, validate deliverables, and coordinate with enterprise
governance boards when required. Smaller offices may leverage enterprise support to fulfill IRB
functions, provided governance responsibilities are clearly assigned and traceable. IRB
composition and cadence may vary based on mission needs, but all boards must uphold IITGF
standards for transparency, responsiveness, and evidence-based decision-making. Mission Areas
seeking guidance on establishing or refining their IRB should reach out to the Enterprise
Governance points of contact for tailored support and examples.

Minor IT Release. A minor release introduces small improvements to the software’s
functionality. However, changes or modifications are made to existing features instead of the
release of new key features. For example, a required patch update. (Source: USDA Definition
of Major and Minor Software Releases)

Non-Major IT Investment. An IT investment that USDA or OMB does not designate as “major.”
A non-major IT investment may be a “standard IT investment,” “IT migration investment,” or
“funding transfer investment.” (Sources: FY 2022 IT Budget – Capital Planning Guidance and
subsequent versions; DR 3130-009)

Project. A temporary endeavor undertaken to create a unique product or service. Projects are
different from other ongoing operations in an organization because, unlike operations, projects
have a definite beginning and an end. That is, they have a limited duration. (Source: USDA
DME and O&M Definitions (and Additional IT-Related Definitions))

Select Phase. A phase of the ITPM process. During this phase, current and potential IT
capabilities are evaluated from a business and technical perspective. This is done to validate
their efficacy and cost relative to potential alternatives. This evaluation is a critical pillar in
conjunction with the agency’s Control and Evaluate phases, which support the continuous
evolution and optimization of the agency’s IT portfolio. (Source: CCA)
