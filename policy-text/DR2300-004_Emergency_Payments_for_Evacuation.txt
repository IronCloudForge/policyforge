1. PURPOSE

     The Departmental Regulation (DR) provides a coordinated and uniform United States
     Department of Agriculture (USDA) policy pursuant to the issuance of emergency payments
     prompted by an order to evacuate from an area threatened by unusual or emergency
     circumstances.


2. SPECIAL INSTRUCTIONS/CANCELLATIONS

     a.   This regulation is effective immediately and supersedes DR 2300-004, USDA
          Evacuation Policies and Procedures, dated March 6, 2006.

     b.   The subject title of this DR has been changed for clarity to “Emergency Payments for
          Evacuation.”
3. SCOPE

  a.   This DR applies to all Mission Areas, agencies, and staff offices.

  b.   This DR primarily addresses the Emergency Payments for Evacuation (EPE) from an
       area threatened by unusual or emergency circumstances.

  c.   This DR provides a coordinated and uniform USDA policy per the issuance from an area
       threatened or emergency circumstances of an order to evacuate and two types of
       payments: Advance Net Salary Payment under 5 Code of Federal Regulations (CFR) §
       550.404, Computation of advance payments and evacuation payments; time periods, and
       Subsistence Payments under 5 CFR § 550.406(c) that may be made to evacuated USDA
       employees and their dependents.

  d.   This DR also describes procedures to calculate the maximum reduction for the meal(s)
       provided due to the age of a dependent or the length in a subsistence and how to
       complete the claim reimbursement form.


4. BACKGROUND

  The USDA may provide emergency payments to employees who are ordered to evacuate
  from their regular worksites and directed to an alternative location, including a home or
  location under quarantine or confinement. The alternative location may be designated as a
  safe haven during the period of evacuation to protect employees from being exposed to
  additional viruses, or mutations of a pandemic virus, without regard to whether the Mission
  Area, agency, or staff office and the employee have a telework agreement in place at the time
  the order to evacuate is issued.


5. POLICY

  Due to the issuance of an order to evacuate, USDA has reviewed the evacuation regulations
  and other applicable laws and has set forth the following two types of payments that may be
  made to evacuated USDA employees and their dependents. These payments consist of
  advance pay and subsistence payments, subject to a few caveats. These are the only
  payments that will be made to USDA employees and their dependents under this authority.

  a.   Advance Net Salary Payments

       USDA may make two types of salary payments:

       (1) One advance payment of an employee’s net salary, under 5 CFR § 550.404, “shall
           cover a time period not to exceed 30 days or a lesser number of days, as determined
           by the authorizing Agency official.” The advance must be approved by the
           authorized Agency official as determined by the Office of Personnel Management



                                                 2
         (OPM). Mission Areas, agencies, and staff offices should refer to OPM regulations
         for information on pay benefits for Federal employees affected by severe weather
         conditions or other emergency situations. The National Finance Center (NFC) will
         process an Electronic Funds Transfer (EFT) payment to the employee’s payroll
         account. If an employee’s financial institution is unable to process the EFT
         payment, the employee may request payment to an alternate EFT account.

     (2) Replacement of salary payments: If the employee’s financial institution is unable to
         process EFT payments, the employee may request payment to an alternate EFT
         account. The unprocessed EFT payment will then be cancelled.

b.   Repayment or Repayment Forgiveness

     (1) At the earliest possible date after the evacuation is terminated (or earlier if the
         circumstances justify), after the employee returns to his or her assigned duty station,
         or when the employee is reassigned officially, the payroll office with jurisdiction
         over the employee's account must review each employee's account for the purpose
         of making adjustments. The employee's pay shall be adjusted based on the rates of
         pay, allowances, or differentials, if any, to which he or she would otherwise have
         been entitled under all applicable statutes other than 5 United States Code (U.S.C.)
         § 5527, Regulations. Any adjustments in the employee's account shall also reflect
         advance payments made to the employee.

     (2) After an employee's account is reviewed, if it is found that the employee is indebted
         for any part of the advance payment made to him or her, or his or her dependent(s)
         or designated representative, recovery of the indebtedness shall be affected by the
         payroll office with jurisdiction over the employee's account, unless a waiver of
         recovery has been approved. Repayment of the indebtedness may be made either in
         full or in partial payments, as determined by the head of the agency or designated
         official.

     (3) Recovery of indebtedness for advance payment shall not be required when it is
         determined by the head of the agency or designated official that the recovery would
         be against equity or good conscience or against the public interest. Findings that
         formed the basis for waiver of recovery shall be filed in the employee's personnel
         folder on the permanent side.

     (4) For the period covered by any payment made under this subpart, the employee shall
         be considered as performing active Federal service in his or her position without a
         break in service.

c.   Subsistence Payments

     (1) Subsistence payments may be provided from the date of departure from the area
         being evacuated to the date of arrival at the designated safe haven in accordance




                                               3
         with 5 CFR § 550.405(a). A designated safe haven is defined as a designated area
         to which an employee or dependent will be or has been evacuated.

     (2) On the date following arrival at the agency designated safe haven, subsistence
         payments may continue until the date that the evacuation order is terminated, or 180
         days after the effective date of the order to evacuate, whichever is earlier. An
         employee must be returned to his/her regular duty station, or appropriate action
         must be taken to reassign him/her to another duty station, as in accordance with 5
         CFR § 550.406(c).

     (3) The maximum per diem, and one-half the maximum per diem for dependents under
         age 12, may be paid to the employee and his or her designated representative for up
         to 30 days. After 30 days, 60 percent of the maximum per diem may be paid for up
         to another 150 days. The per diem rates are set by the General Services
         Administration (GSA). An example of the per diem rates and percentages paid are
         provided in detail below.

     (4) Supervisors or higher-level officials may authorize subsistence payments to
         employees and their dependents. Payments should be authorized (for both
         employees and dependents) in the Concur Government Edition system used by
         USDA.

d.   Additional Information and Restrictions

     (1) Transportation: Travel expenses for the employee and dependents will be
         determined, in accordance with the Federal Travel Regulation (FTR), from the
         evacuated area to the designated safe haven and for their return to the previously
         evacuated area, as authorized.

     (2) Lodging-plus: Actual amount of lodging plus a set allowance for meals and
         expenses (per diem). USDA will pay actual lodging costs up to 300% plus the
         Meal and Incidental Expense (M&IE) at the per diem amount.

     (3) Per Diem Percentages Allowed:

         (a) Adult 1st 30 days: Employees and dependents that are 12 years and older are
             entitled to 100 percent of the maximum per diem.

         (b) Adult 31-180 days: Employees and dependents that are 12 years and older are
             entitled to 60 percent of the maximum per diem.

         (c) Children 1st 30 days: Dependents under the age of 12 are entitled to 50
             percent of the maximum per diem.

         (d) Children 31-180 days: Dependents under the age of 12 are entitled to 30
             percent of the maximum per diem.

    regulations a dependent is a relative of the employee residing with the employee
    and dependent on the employee for support.

(5) Vouchering:

    (a) Employees: Employees should receive travel authorizations for their personal
        subsistence expenses. Vouchers should be completed using the normal travel
        voucher processes.

         1   Consult your supervisor or administrative staff to determine travel
             authorization and voucher processes.

         2   USDA’s Concur Government Edition should be used to submit, authorize,
             and voucher travel expenses.

         3   Note that the authorizations should be completed to allow only those
             expenses authorized by 5 CFR § 550.405.

    (b) Dependents: Subsistence expenses will be reimbursed using a GSA Optional
        Form (OF)-1164, Claim for Reimbursement for Expenditures on Official
        Business. The OF-1164 must be approved by the employee’s supervisor or
        designated authority.

(6) Paying Subsistence Expenses (see Example Scenario in Appendix A):

    (a) Prepaid card (dependent expenses only)

         1   Prepaid cards are limited to the balance authorized.

         2   Prepaid cards can include the subsistence payments of dependents.
             Employees should use the travel card or travel advance processes.

         3   The amount issued on the Prepaid card must be subtracted from the
             reimbursement requested on an OF-1164. See page D-1 of Appendix D,
             Example Scenario, for the recommended format.

         4   Supervisors or a designated authority may request Prepaid cards through
             the Office of the Chief Financial Officer’s (OCFO) Financial Management
             Service at (800) 1-800-421-0323 from 8 a.m. to 8 p.m. central time.

e.   Restrictions

     (1) Receipts documenting actual lodging and transportation costs will be required. If
         receipts are unavailable, supervisors can accept alternate documentation at their
         discretion. (Example: Employee lost receipt due to the need to evacuate,
         documentation of the charges from US Bank billing statements can be used.)

     (2) Subsistence per diem can be used for any purpose. If lodging expenses are not
         charged to the employee (or family), no lodging reimbursement will be provided.
         This includes lodging provided by evacuation shelters, family, and charities.

     (3) If the employee leaves the designated safe haven or performs unofficial temporary
         duty travel, subsistence payments to the employee will be suspended for this period.
         Payments will continue for dependents that remain at the designated safe haven and
         will resume for the employee when they return to the Mission Area, agency, or staff
         office designated safe haven. Administrative or sick leave does not interrupt
         subsistence payments.

     (4) If dependents of the employee leave the designated safe haven, subsistence
         payments will be suspended for this period. Payments will resume once the
         dependent returns to the designated safe haven. Payments will continue for the
         employee and dependents that remain at the designated safe haven.

     (5) Subsistence payments will terminate for both the employee and dependents when
         the evacuation is terminated or when an employee is assigned or returned to a
         permanent duty station (relocation).

6. ROLES AND RESPONSIBILITIES

   a.   The Senior Accountable Travel Officer (SATO), or a delegate officer, will review
        payments and approve the payments.

   b.   NFC personnel will process EFT payment to the employee’s payroll account.

   c.   Mission Area, agency, and staff office Payroll Office Staff will review each employee’s
        account for the making adjustments after evaluation.

   d.   Mission Area, agency, and staff office Directors, or a delegated officer, and Supervisors
        will communicate regularly with their employees who are performing work during
        emergencies to ensure they understand their work assignments and management's
        expectations during the period of the evacuation.


7. INQUIRIES

   All USDA Mission Areas, agencies, and staff offices may direct questions and inquiries
   regarding this DR 2300-004 to the OCFO, Fiscal Policy Division (FPD) via email at
   TPM@usda.gov.

                                         DEFINITIONS


Advance Net Salary Payments. The amount of advance payments shall cover a time period not
to exceed 30 days or a lesser number of days, as determined by the authorizing agency official.
(Source: 5 CFR part 550, Subpart D)

Agency. Organizational units of the Department, other than staff offices as defined below,
whose head reports to officials within the Office of the Secretary, Deputy Secretary, Under
Secretaries, Assistant Secretaries, and Assistant to the Secretary. (Source: 5 CFR § 550.402)

Mission Area. A group of agencies with related functions that report to the same Under or
Assistant Secretary. Research, Education, and Economics (REE) is an example of a Mission
Area. (Source: USDA, DR 0100-001, Departmental Directives System)

National Finance Center (NFC). The organization that processes Electronic Funds Transfer
(EFT) payments to the employee’s payroll account. (Source: National Finance Center website)

Repayment Forgiveness. After the employee returns to his or her assigned duty station, the
payroll office having jurisdiction over the employee's account, review each employee's account
for the purpose of adjusting. (Source: 5 CFR part 550, Subpart D)

Replacement of salary payments. Financial institution is unable to process EFT payments, the
employee may request payment to an alternate EFT account or may request a Prepaid Card.
(Source: 5 CFR part 550, Subpart D)

Safe haven. The area to which an employee or dependent will be or has been evacuated.
(Source: 5 CFR § 550.402)

Staff Office. Departmental administrative offices whose heads report to officials within the
Office of the Secretary. (Source: USDA, DR 0100-001)

Subsistence Payments. The payments provided from the date of departure from the area being
evacuated to the date of arrival at the agency designated safe haven, including any period of
delay enroute that is beyond an evacuee’s control. (Source: 5 CFR Part 550, Subpart D)

1. Employee is evacuated to the Location 6 safe haven. Travel from the evacuation area begins
   on 2/15/2021 and ends on 2/16/2021. Transportation expenses of three hundred dollars
   ($300) were incurred (mileage of personally owned vehicle). Subsistence at the designated
   safe haven begins 2/17/2021.

2. On the evening of 2/15/2021, the employee and dependents (spouse and three children ages
   11, 12, and 16) stay at a relative or friend’s house (cost $0); 2/16/2021 was spent at a motel
   (two rooms for a total of $125). The employee receives a Prepaid card worth three thousand
   dollars ($3,000) as an advance for the family’s subsistence expenses.

3. The employee or family stay at the agency designated a safe haven location for 81 days. The
   family rents an apartment for two thousand dollars ($2,000) per month. During that period,
   some family members leave according to the schedule below:

   a.   Days 7 – 12: Employee performs official travel to Washington, DC.

   b.   Days 31 – 52: The three children visit grandparents in Portland, Oregon, (outside the
        designated safe haven area).

   c.   Days 31 – 40: Employee and spouse travel back to permanent duty station to survey the
        damage to their personal property.

   are provided at no cost to the employee and dependents:

   a.   Evacuation day #1 (2/15/2021); Red Cross provided breakfast and lunch.

   b.   Day #2 at the designated safe haven location (2/18/2021); the employer provides dinner
        for all employees and their dependents.

   c.   Day #5 at designated safe haven (2/21/2021); the employer provides lunch at the
        alternate worksite. Employee had lunch, but dependents were not provided lunch.
