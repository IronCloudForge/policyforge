1. PURPOSE

   This Departmental Regulation (DR) establishes the United States Department of
   Agriculture’s (USDA) policy for sustainable operations of real property, personal property,
   and acquisitions.


2. SPECIAL INSTRUCTIONS/CANCELLATIONS

   a.     This DR supersedes Section 5b, Sustainable Operations, of DR 5600-005,
          Environmental Management, dated September 24, 2012.

   b.     A forthcoming revision to DR 5600-005, Environmental Management will supersede the
          balance of that directive.
  c.   This DR is in effect upon issue and remains in effect until it is superseded or expires.


3. SCOPE

  This DR applies to all USDA Mission Areas, agencies, and staff offices involved in
  acquisitions as well as those responsible for the design, construction, leasing, operations,
  maintenance, or disposition of real or personal property.


4. BACKGROUND

  a.   Multiple statutes require Federal agencies to reduce their environmental impacts, reduce
       energy and water use, increase the use of renewable energy, purchase sustainable
       products, and meet other related goals.

  b.   USDA takes action to design and construct buildings, to conduct operations, and to
       procure, manage, and dispose of real and personal property, including fleet, sustainably.
       It does so to reduce direct and indirect impacts to the environment, increase operational
       efficiencies, and promote the health and well-being of USDA employees and visitors.


5. POLICY

  a.   USDA complies with all Federal, state, and local requirements including laws, policies
       and E.O.s, as they apply to sustainable operations. These include energy,
       environmental, sustainable building, and procurement mandates.

  b.   The USDA Mission Areas, agencies, and staff offices must, across their portfolios:

       (1) Reduce building energy use intensity annually;

       (2) Use renewable energy and consume energy efficiently;

       (3) Reduce water use intensity;

       (4) Manage stormwater effects;

       (5) Use performance contracts to attain USDA energy and water goals;

       (6) Sustainably design, construct, and operate new and existing buildings and
           landscaping, to protect the environment and human health, and ensure operational
           resiliency;



                                                2
       (7) Track and report on actions to improve performance for energy, water, greenhouse
           gas emissions, costs, and for other metrics as required;

       (8) Consider building and site efficiencies in leasing actions;

       (9) Optimize space in owned and leased facility practices, in keeping with occupant
           safety and health requirements;

       (10) Use sustainable practices to manage and operate the USDA fleet and to reduce
            petroleum use;

       (11) Prevent waste and improve recycling practices;

       (12) Comply with all Federal solid, hazardous, and toxic waste disposal mandates;

       (13) Follow required purchasing preferences and acquire, use, and dispose of products
            and services, including electronics, sustainably through the lifecycle; and

       (14) Use Environmental Management Systems (EMS) where appropriate. Implement
            and maintain effective management tools.


6. ROLES AND RESPONSIBILITIES

  a.   The USDA Assistant Secretary for Administration (ASA) holds primary responsibility
       for USDA sustainable practices. The ASA will:

       (1) Represent USDA on Federal interagency sustainability steering committees;

       (2) Chair periodic USDA Sustainable Operations Council (SOC) meetings;

       (3) Develop a strategic USDA process to ensure that functions and programs include
           sustainability goals;

       (4) Ensure that USDA Mission Areas, agencies, and staff offices integrate sustainable
           practices into all policies, programs, plans and accountability wherever relevant;

       (5) Prepare and distribute USDA Mission Area, agency, and staff office scorecards to
           recognize goal attainment and identify areas for improvement; and

       (6) Provide the USDA reports and plans to the Office of Management and Budget
           (OMB), Council on Environmental Quality (CEQ), and lead Federal agencies.

  b.   The Director of Office of Property and Environmental Management (OPEM) will:


                                               3
     (1) Lead USDA sustainable operations programs;

     (2) Develop goals, objectives, and targets for SOC approval;

     (3) Direct staff to develop and carry out policies;

     (4) Issue data calls and direct staff to write, review, analyze, and submit required
         Mission Area, agency, and staff office reports;

     (5) Monitor progress in meeting the USDA goals; and

     (6) Assist, guide, and direct USDA Mission Areas, agencies, and staff offices to set
         policies and practices specified in this DR.

c.   Mission Area, Agency, and Staff Office Heads will:

     (1) Participate in the USDA SOC;

     (2) Identify Mission Area, agency, or staff office liaisons to prepare the Mission Area,
         agency, or staff office’s annual reports, plans, and OMB scorecard data;

     (3) Integrate sustainable operations within each Mission Area, agency, or staff office's
         strategic planning, budgeting process, and, if present, EMS;

     (4) Identify, develop, and complete relevant Mission Area, agency, or staff office
         activities, policies, plans, procedures, and practices;

     (5) Specify Mission Area, agency, or staff office goals, schedules, milestones, and
         means to achieve the USDA goals as well as to align with statutory and regulatory
         requirements;

     (6) Attain energy, water, sustainable buildings, low impact development, waste
         management, electronics stewardship, procurement, fleet management and all other
         sustainable practices program goals; and

     (7) Track Mission Area, agency, or staff office progress towards meeting these goals.

d.   Employees will:

     Follow practices and take actions to achieve their Mission Area, agency, or staff office’s
     sustainable operations objectives and targets in keeping with the Federal and USDA
     goals and policies.

                                         DEFINITIONS


Agency. Organizational units of the Department, other than staff offices as defined below,
whose heads report to officials within the Office of the Secretary, Deputy Secretary, Under
Secretaries, Assistant Secretaries, and Assistant to the Secretary. (Source: USDA, DR 0100001,
Departmental Directives System)

Biobased product. As determined by the U.S. Department of Agriculture, a commercial or
industrial product, other than food or feed, that is composed, in whole or significant part, of
biological products, including renewable domestic agricultural materials, renewable chemicals
and forestry materials. Learn more at www.biopreferred.gov.

Environmental Management System (EMS). A framework that helps an organization achieve its
environmental goals through consistent review, evaluation, and continual improvement of its
environmental performance. An EMS uses the “Plan-Do-Check-Act” framework for continual
improvement. The most widely accepted model is the International Organization for
Standardization’s ISO 14001 standard (current edition).

Greenhouse Gases (GHG). Gases that trap heat in the atmosphere. These gases affect processes
of the ocean and atmosphere. The principal GHGs that enter the atmosphere because of human
activities include carbon dioxide, methane, nitrous oxide, and certain fluorinated gases.

Mission Area. A group of agencies with related functions that report to the same Under or
Assistant Secretary. Research, Education, and Economics (REE) is an example of a Mission
Area. (Source: USDA, DR 0100-001)

OMB Scorecard. An OMB developed tool. The CEQ and OMB use scorecards to evaluate
agency progress in meeting sustainable operations goals specified in statutes and E.O.s.

Renewable Energy. Energy generated from solar, wind, biomass, landfill gas, ocean (including
tidal, wave, current, and thermal), geothermal, municipal solid waste, or new hydroelectric
generation capacity achieved from increased efficiency or additions of new capacity at an
existing hydroelectric project. (Source: 42 United States Code (U.S.C.) § 15852(b)(2),
Renewable energy).

Staff Office. Departmental administrative offices whose heads report to officials within the
Office of the Secretary. (Source: USDA, DR 0100-001)

                                         APPENDIX B


Sustainability. Within an environmental, societal, and economic context, the capacity to create
and maintain conditions under which humans and nature can exist in productive harmony.
Sustainable development meets the needs of current generations without harming the ability of
future generations to meet their own needs. (Sources: The Environmental Protection Agency
(EPA) and the Brundtland Commission)
