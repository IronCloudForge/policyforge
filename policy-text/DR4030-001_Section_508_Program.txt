1. PURPOSE

   a) This Departmental Regulation (DR) establishes the United States Department of
      Agriculture (USDA) policy for the implementation of Section 508 of the Rehabilitation
      Act of 1973, 29 United States Code (U.S.C.) § 794d, as amended. Section 508 of the
      Rehabilitation Act applies to all Federal agencies when developing, procuring,
      maintaining, or using information and communication technology (ICT).

   b) USDA is committed to ensuring that employees, job applicants, and members of the
      public with disabilities have access to ICT comparable to the access available to those
      without disabilities.
2. SCOPE

  This DR applies to all USDA Mission Areas, agencies, staff offices, employees, appointees,
  and contractors performing work for, or on behalf of, USDA. All ICT developed, procured,
  funded, maintained, and used by USDA must be accessible to individuals with disabilities,
  including employees, job applicants, and members of the public seeking information or
  services from the agency, in accordance with Section 508 of the Rehabilitation Act of 1973,
  as amended.


3. SPECIAL INSTRUCTIONS/CANCELLATIONS

  a) This DR supersedes DR 4030-001, Section 508 Program, dated April 8, 2014.

  b) Mission Areas, agencies, and staff offices will align their policies and procedures with
     this DR within 6 months of the publication date.


4. BACKGROUND

  a) In 1998, Congress amended the Rehabilitation Act of 1973 to require Federal agencies to
     make their electronic information and technology (EIT) accessible to people with
     disabilities. Under Section 508, agencies must give individuals with disabilities,
     including employees, job applicants, and members of the public, access to information
     comparable to the access available to others.

      The U.S. Access Board (“Access Board”) is responsible for developing accessibility
      standards. In 2017, the U.S. Access Board published a Final Rule that that updated
      accessibility requirements covered by Section 508 and refreshed guidelines in the law.
      The Final Rule went into effect on January 18, 2018. The original Section 508 term EIT
      was replaced with Information and Communication Technology (ICT), to reflect new
      technologies. Categories of ICT include electronic content, hardware, software, and
      support documentation and services. The regulations can be found at 36 CFR Part 1194.

  b) The Access Board Section 508 standards apply to procurements by the Federal
     Government. This includes but is not limited to computer hardware and software,
     websites, phone systems, and copiers. Section 508 of the Rehabilitation Act requires that
     all ICT developed, procured, funded, maintained, and used by Federal agencies be
     accessible to individuals with disabilities, including employees, and members of the
     public seeking information or services from the agency.

       1) Refresh of the Section 508 standards and the Communications Act of 1934, 47
          U.S.C. § 255, Access by Persons with Disabilities

           (a) On January 18, 2017, the Access Board published the Final Rule, Information
               and Communication Technology (ICT) Standards and Guidelines. The Final
               Rule updates the Section 508 standards along with the accessibility guidelines


                                              2
        for telecommunication products and equipment covered by Section 255 of the
        Communications Act. The Access Board standards are available at Information
        and Communication Technology: Revised 508 Standards and 255 Guidelines.

   (b) The Final Rule updated and reorganized the Section 508 standards and Section
       255 guidelines in response to market trends and innovations, such as
       significant advancements in technology. The Final Rule also harmonized these
       requirements with other guidelines and standards both in the U.S. and abroad.
       These include European Telecommunications Standards Institute (ETSI)
       accessibility standards EN 301 549, V3.2.1, Accessibility Requirements for ICT
       Products and Services, for public ICT procurement. They also include the
       World Wide Web Consortium (W3C) Web Content Accessibility Guidelines
       (WCAG) 2.1 and the approved Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG)
       2.2, a globally recognized voluntary consensus standard for web content and
       ICT.

2) Coverage of electronic content (Section 508 standards)

   Similar to the original Section 508 standards, the updated Section 508 standards
   apply to a Federal agency’s full range of public-facing content. This includes, but
   is not limited to websites, documents and media, blog posts, and social media sites.
   The Final Rule specifically lists the types of non-public-facing content that must
   comply. The list includes but is not limited to:

   (a) Electronic content used by a Federal agency for official business to
       communicate emergency notifications;

   (b) Initial or final decisions adjudicating administrative claims or proceedings;

   (c) Internal or external program or policy announcements;

   (d) Notices of benefits, program eligibility, employment opportunity, or personnel
       action;

   (e) Formal acknowledgements or receipts;

   (f) Questionnaires or surveys;

   (g) Templates or forms;

   (h) Educational or training materials; and

   (i) Web-based intranets.

5. POLICY

  USDA is committed to making its ICT accessible to all people, including individuals with
  disabilities. Compliance with the Section 508 standards is mandatory for Federal agencies.
  This policy establishes the guidance and responsibilities for implementing and maintaining
  an ICT accessibility program to ensure the following:

  a) Employees and job applicants with disabilities have access to ICT that is comparable to
     the access and use by employees and job applicants who do not have disabilities, unless it
     would impose an undue burden on the agency.

  b) Individuals with disabilities, who are members of the public seeking information or
     services from the agency, have access to and use of USDA information and services that
     is comparable to that provided to the public who are not individuals with disabilities,
     unless an undue burden would be imposed on the agency.

  c) All ICT systems or products that provide direct services to the public or Federal
     employees are accessible to individuals with disabilities. This does not apply only if a
     formal request for an exception from an established Section 508 standard is granted. The
     procedures can be found in the Departmental Manual (DM) 4030-001, Section 508
     Procedures.

  d) All USDA Mission Areas, agencies, and staff offices will work with the USDA Section
     508 Program Office, within the Office of the Chief Information Officer (OCIO), to
     establish their Section 508 program within six months of the publication date of this DR.

  e) All USDA Mission Areas, agencies, and staff offices will establish information
     technology (IT) procurement guidance for Section 508 compliant solicitations for all ICT,
     per this DR and the Federal Acquisition Regulations (FAR) 48 Code of Federal
     Regulations (CFR) Part 39, Acquisition of Information Technology. This will occur
     within 3 months of the publication date of this DR.

  f) All USDA Mission Areas, agencies, and staff offices will appoint a Section 508
     Coordinator within 2 months of the publication of this DR.

       1) The USDA Section 508 Program Office will provide coordinators with the criteria
          and training requirements needed for a person to serve as a Section 508
          Coordinator.

       2) Evidence of the appointment will be documented in writing and submitted annually
          to the USDA Section 508 Program Office.

  g) Section 508 Coordinators are responsible for managing their respective Mission Area,
     agency, or staff office Section 508 programs. This includes developing and maintaining
     Section 508 policies, guidance, and other best practice efforts on behalf of their program
     office.

6. SECTION 508 TRAINING AND AWARENESS

  a) All current and new USDA employees and contractors must complete Section 508
     mandatory training annually. The training provides a general overview of the Section
     508 requirements, USDA Section 508 responsibilities, and product delivery service
     requirements for employees and members of the public.

  b) Additionally, all USDA Section 508 Coordinators and employees performing in roles
     pertinent to the Section 508 provisions must also complete specialized Section 508
     training. This includes Section 508 accessibility webinars, Trusted Tester training,
     document remediation training, and quality assurance training for ICT and electronic
     content.

  c) The AgLearn training system will annually notify employees and contractors when they
     must complete the mandatory Section 508 training. The USDA Section 508 Program
     Office will report metrics pertaining to training completion to the Office of Management
     and Budget (OMB), the General Services Administration (GSA), and the Access Board.

  d) Continuing education is a requirement for USDA’s Section 508 Program Manager (PM)
     and all Section 508 Coordinators. This ensures that they can comply with laws and stay
     current with the latest accessibility developments, issues, skills, and new technologies.


7. SECTION 508 COMPLIANCE

  a) USDA employees are responsible for working with their Mission Area, agency, or staff
     office Section 508 Coordinator to ensure that their ICT is accessible to individuals with
     disabilities. If a Mission Area, agency, or staff office does not have a designated Section
     508 Coordinator, contact the USDA Section 508 Program Office for assistance at
     Section508-ocio@usda.gov.

  b) To the greatest extent possible, Mission Areas, agencies, and staff offices must ensure
     that their ICT, regardless of platform or media, meets the technical provisions for
     accessibility as prescribed by the Access Board.

  c) If the agency determines that acquiring ICT that meets the technical provisions of Section
     508 would impose an undue burden or an exception, the agency still has an obligation to
     make information and data available by an alternative means.

  d)   Mission Areas, agencies, and staff offices must ensure the development of a plan for risk
       management, risk assessments and the documentation of buying, implementing,
       developing, maintaining, and using ICT products that do not meet the Access Board’s
       technical standards.

8. SECTION 508 COMPLAINT PROCEDURES

  Section 508 of the Rehabilitation Act of 1973 is a law that requires federal agencies to ensure
  that their ICT is accessible to people with disabilities. If you need to report a Section 508
  violation or need to request accessibility to digital content, the administrative process is as
  follows:

  a) Submit your administrative violation to the Section 508 Program Manager at Section508-
     ocio@usda.gov describing the ICT that is inaccessible, the Staff Office or Mission Area
     that is responsible for the content, and the impact on your ability to access the
     information or services.

  b) Provide your contact information and any supporting information or documents.

  c) The Section 508 Program Office will review the administrative violation or accessibility
     request within a reasonable timeframe. You may also contact the Staff Office or Mission
     Area Section 508 Coordinator for their assistance and guidance. For more information,
     you can visit the Section 508 website.

  d) Complaints of discrimination regarding Section 508 accessibility should be filed with the
     Office of the Assistant Secretary for Civil Rights. A complainant may file a
     complaint online via the Program Discrimination Complaint Portal Complaint Form
     Portal; by completing the USDA Program Discrimination Complaint Form (English), at
     any USDA office by calling (866)- 632-9992, or sending an email/letter
     to program.intake@usda.gov.


9. SECTION 508 MANDATORY REQUIREMENTS

  a) The Access Board’s technical provisions are mandatory requirements that must be met
     directly or through substantially equivalent or greater access. This is also known as
     equivalent facilitation. The USDA Section 508 Official may approve a request for an
     exception.

  b) USDA will ensure that all ICT meets the revised requirements of Section 508 and the
     FAR. Specifically:




                                               6
       1) USDA must provide the applicable Section 508 provisions in contract language to
          all contractors.

       2) Conformance to the Section 508 standards must persist whenever there are upgrades
          (system or application), patches, and alterations to ICT products and services. This
          includes, but is not limited to, websites, software systems, documents, emails,
          training materials, and multimedia.

       3) All internal and external emails and their attachments must have meaningful links
          that are accessible to recipients with disabilities.

       4) Broadcasted emails must comply with the applicable Section 508 standards. They
          may not substitute individual accommodations for Section 508 compliance.

       5) Disseminated working draft documents should meet the Section 508 standards as
          much as practicable. Alternatively, accessible formats or an equivalent facilitation
          must be made available upon request.

      All contracts and purchase agreements for the procurement of ICT must include all
      necessary provisions for Section 508 compliance. This is mandatory unless an
      exception was granted in accordance with established Section 508 standards and USDA
      procedures. These procedures can be found in the Section 508 Procedures.

  c) Contracting officers and individual credit card purchasers must acquire ICT that meets
     the applicable technical provisions to the maximum extent practicable. Solicitations for
     ICT should be drafted so that personnel may consider products offering equivalent
     facilitation, along with those that strictly meet the technical provisions in the Access
     Board’s standards.


10. SECTION 508 PRODUCT EVALUATION AND REMEDIATION

  a) All newly developed or procured ICT and pre-existing ICT that USDA maintains and
     uses will undergo assessment for compliance. This must occur in accordance with the
     Access Board and the Section 508 Reference Procedures.

  b) All solicitation which includes ICT must specify accessibility requirements.

  c) Vendors must create an Accessibility Conformance Report (ACR) using the current GSA
     Voluntary Product Accessibility Template (VPAT®) for all ICT developed or procured by
     USDA. Vendors must provide the report to the Mission Area, agency, or staff office
     Section 508 Coordinator prior to deployment.

  d) All USDA employees and contractors performing work for, or on behalf of, USDA must
     report to the Section 508 Coordinator all pre-existing and legacy ICT operating and




                                              7
     maintained in a production environment without a Section 508 compliance determination.
     The Section 508 Coordinator will perform quality assurance testing and validation.

  e) Only ICT that has been appropriately reviewed and determined to be Section 508
     compliant, or that has been granted an exception in accordance with procedures in the
     Section 508 Procedures, will be placed into production. All ICT operating in a
     production environment that is noncompliant with Section 508 must have a remediation
     plan for achieving compliance or a request for an exception prepared and submitted to the
     USDA Section 508 Program Office within 45 business days of the noncompliance
     determination. Submit requests to Section508-ocio@usda.gov.

                                          DEFINITIONS


Access Board. An independent Federal agency that promotes equality for individuals with
disabilities through leadership in accessible design and the development of accessibility
guidelines and standards. Also known as the Architectural and Transportation Barriers
Compliance Board. (Source: Access Board, About the U.S. Access Board).

Accessibility Conformance Report (ACR). ACR is a representation of how the product meets
the applicable Section 508 technical standards. The product owner or developer must first test
the product against those standards in order to complete an ACR. Currently, the most common
way to complete an ACR is by using the Voluntary Product Accessibility Template (VPAT®)
template created by the ITIC. (Source: GSA, Section508.gov, How to Create an Accessibility
Conformance Report Using A Voluntary Product Accessibility Template (VPAT®)).

Accessibility Requirements Tool (ART). A step-by-step guide to help easily identify relevant
accessibility requirements from the revised Section 508 standards (see the Access Board,
Information and Communication Technology: Revised 508 Standards and 255 Guidelines) and
incorporate them into procurement and contracting documentation, as well as in-house IT
development. The GSA’s IT Accessibility Program developed ART as part of its mission to
provide technical assistance to Federal agencies, to improve compliance with Section 508 of the
Rehabilitation Act. (Source: GSA, IT Accessibility Laws and Policies).

AgMax. The acquisition approval request system used by USDA.

Equivalent Facilitations. The use of designs, products, or technologies as alternatives to those
prescribed, resulting in substantially equivalent or greater accessibility and usability. (Source:
Access Board, Information and Communication Technology: Revised 508 Standards and 255
Guidelines).

Federal Acquisition Regulations (FAR). A set of principles that govern the Government
procurement process. The FAR regulates purchasing of goods and services by the Government.
(Source: GSA, Federal Acquisition Regulation).

Final Rule (for the purposes of this DR). The Access Board’s Final Rule updated requirements
for ICT covered by Section 508 of the Rehabilitation Act and Section 255 of the
Communications Act. The revisions and updates to the Section 508-based standards and Section
255-based guidelines are intended to ensure that information and communication technology
covered by the respective statutes is accessible to and usable by individuals with disabilities.
(Source: Federal Register, Final Rule, Information and Communication Technology (ICT)
Standards and Guidelines).

Information and Communication Technology (ICT). A term for IT that stresses the role of
unified communications and the integration of telecommunications (telephone lines and wireless



                                                B-1
signals) and computers, as well as necessary enterprise software, middleware, storage, and all
EIT. (Source: Access Board, Information and Communication Technology: Revised 508
Standards and 255 Guidelines).

Section 508 of the Rehabilitation Act of 1973, as amended. This is a Federal law that requires
Federal agencies to provide individuals with disabilities equal access to electronic information
and data comparable to those who do not have disabilities, unless an undue burden would be
imposed on the Federal agency. (Source: Access Board, Section 508 (Federal Electronic and
Information Technology)).

Trusted Tester. The Department of Homeland Security Trusted Tester process is a manual test
approach that aligns with the ICT Testing Baseline and provides repeatable and reliable
conformance test results. (Source: GSA, Trusted Tester and ICT Testing Baseline).

Undue Burden. The undue burden exception applies only to the specific features or functions of
the ICT that cannot be made to conform to the Revised 508 Standards without imposing an
undue burden on the agency or component. (Source: Step 2: Determine ICT Exceptions).

Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG). The WCAG defines how to make web content
more accessible to individuals with disabilities. (Source: W3C, Web Content Accessibility
Guidelines (WCAG) 2.2).

Working Draft Disseminations. Living documents where multiple people are editing the
document before the final version. These documents are typically sent via email for updates,
edits, and corrections. (Source: W3C, Types of Documents W3C Publishes).
