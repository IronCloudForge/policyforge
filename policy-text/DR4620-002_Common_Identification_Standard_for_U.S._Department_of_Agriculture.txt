24   1. PURPOSE
25
26        This Departmental Regulation (DR) prescribes the credentialing standards, policies, roles,
27        and responsibilities for the implementation of Homeland Security Presidential Directive
28        (HSPD) 12, Policy for a Common Identification Standard for Federal Employees and
29        Contractors, as well as the issuance of alternative identity credentials for short-term, HSPD-
30        12 exempt, or HSPD-12 ineligible populations requiring access to federally controlled
31        facilities and/or federally controlled information systems.
32
33   2. SCOPE
34
35        This DR applies to all Mission Areas, agencies, and staff offices.
36

37   4. BACKGROUND
38
39     a.   HSPD-12 establishes the general requirements for a common Federal identification
40          system. The President has mandated that all Federal departments provide a process for
41          identity proofing and credentialing employees and contractors to increase security and
42          provide greater interoperability between departments and Federal facilities.
43     b.   For further information on HSPD-12 and its related requirements and standards, please
44          see sources in Appendix C.

46   5. POLICY



                                                      2
 1
 2   This policy is established to enhance security, increase Government efficiency, reduce
 3   identity fraud, and protect personal privacy by establishing a mandatory, USDA-wide
 4   standard for secure and reliable forms of identification to its employees and contractors.
 5
 6   a.   Departmental Mission Areas, agencies, and staff offices must comply with HSPD-12 for
 7        all applicable USDA federal employees and non-Federal employees. Detailed
 8        procedures are described in DM 4620-002.
 9
10   b.   The issuance of the HSPD-12 compliant PIV credential, referred to now as the USDA
11        LincPass is mandatory and applies to:
12        (1) All federal employees, as defined in 5 United States Code (U.S.C.) §2105,
13            “Employee,” within a department or agency whose employment is likely to
14            continue for at least six (6) continuous months and requires physical and/or logical
15            access to federally controlled facilities and/or information systems.
16        (2) All other categories of persons, referred to now as non-Federal employees,
17            performing work for or on behalf of the USDA, such as contractors, grantees,
18            interns, volunteers, affiliates, licensees, certificate holders, fellows, and guest
19            researchers whose engagement is likely to continue for at least six (6) continuous
20            months and requires physical and/or logical access to federally controlled facilities
21            and/or information systems.
22
23   c.   Federal employees and non-Federal employees whose term is less than six (6)
24        continuous months are considered short-term and are eligible for alternative credentials
25        based on the Mission Area, agency, or staff office risk-based decision and minimum
26        vetting standards outlined in DM4620-002.
27
28   d.   Intermittent employees with affiliation that extends beyond six continuous months are
29        not considered “short-term.” Similarly, seasonal employees, i.e., employees on
30        permanent appointments who work a seasonal schedule of six months or more on a
31        recurring basis are not considered “short-term”.
32
33   e.   Non-U.S. nationals who cannot meet the investigative standards for PIV may be eligible
34        for alternative identity credentials based on Mission Area, agency, or staff office risk-
35        based decision and the Office of Personnel Management (OPM) Credentialing Executive
36        Agent (CredEA) investigative standards for non-U.S. nationals.
37
38   f.   Alternative credentials can provide logical and/or physical access but do not meet the
39        requirements of HSPD-12 and are visually and electronically distinguishable from the
40        LincPass credential. Alternative credential and risk-based decision guidance is found in
41        the DM-4620-002.
42
43   g.   Federal employees as defined by 5 U.S.C § 2105, and non-Federal employees with
44        USDA will be required to follow procedures in FIPS 201-3, the NIST Special
45        Publication (SP) series related to HSPD-12, and DM 4620-002 for the issuance of a
46        LincPass credential.

 7
 8     a.   The Director of OSSP will:
 9
10          (1) Develop, in consultation with the USDA Office of Homeland Security (OHS) and
11              the OCIO, policies, standards, and procedures for implementing and administering
12              the HSPD-12 program throughout the Department;
13
14          (2) Work with Mission Areas, agencies, and staff offices to maintain policies and
15              procedures to support the identity proofing, registration, and credentialing of
16              employees;
17
18          (3) Maintain an enterprise Physical Access Control System (ePACS) environment to
19              support Mission Area, agency, and staff office physical access control systems;
20
21          (4) Oversee and assist with migrating all physical access control systems into HSPD-12
22              compliance;
23
24          (5) Support role holder training module development;
25

27              responsibilities for managing non-Federal employees in accordance with DM 4620-
28              002; and
29
30          (7) Set the standards for the hosting of credentialing stations within USDA that follow
31              or exceed USAccess requirements.

34              Office compliance with credentialing standards.

44              3551, et seq.);

 3                                         DEFINITIONS
 4
 5
 6   Access Control. The process of granting or denying requests to access physical facilities or
 7   areas, or to logical systems (e.g., computer networks or software applications). See also
 8   “logical access control system” and “physical access control system.”
 9
10   Access (Routine). Entry into and use of a facility and/or information system on a regular
11   basis without an escort and/or continuous monitoring by a USDA official. The Mission Area,
12   agency, and staff office determination should be based upon the support to successfully
13   complete USDA’s mission functions. Routine access requires a mandatory PIV or PIV-I
14   credential to be issued.
15
16   Access (Unaccompanied). Entry into and use of a facility and/or information system without
17   an escort and/or continuous monitoring by a USDA official. The Mission Area, agency, and
18   staff office’s determination for unaccompanied access should be based upon the support to
19   successfully complete USDA’s mission functions. Unaccompanied access requires a
20   mandatory LincPass to be issued.
21
22   AltLinc (PIV-I). USDA has named their common identity PIV-I card the AltLinc, as it is
23   designed to link a person’s identity to an identification card and the card to a person’s ability
24   to access Federal buildings and computer systems. This card will be used to service short-
25   term personnel (less than 6 months) and personnel who fail to meet the HSPD-12 minimum
26   investigative requirements. The spelling of the AltLinc PIV-I card is derived from the word
27   alternative as it is meant to serve as an alternative to the LincPass.
28
29   Contractor. An individual under contract to USDA (for the purpose of HSPD-12
30   implementation).
31
32   Credential. An identity card (“smart card”), also known as LincPass or AltLinc, that contains
33   stored identity credentials. The card is issued to an individual and the claimed identity of the
34   cardholder can be verified against stored credentials by another person or by an automated
35   process. Other approved forms of a credential may be issued when applicable.
36
37   Employee. Defined in 5 U.S.C. § 2105, “Employee,” within a department or Mission Area,
38   agency, and staff office. “Employee” means a person, other than the President and Vice
39   President, employed by, detailed or assigned to, USDA, including members of the Armed
40   Forces; an expert or consultant to USDA; an industrial or commercial contractor, licensee,
41   certificate holder, or grantee of USDA, including all subcontractors; or any other category of
42   person who acts on behalf of an Mission Area, agency, and staff office as determined by the
43   Mission Area, agency, and staff office head.
44
45   Federal Facility. The term “Federal facility” means any building, structure, or fixture or part
46   thereof which is owned by the United States or any Federal agency or which is held by the



                                                  B-1
 1   United States or any Federal agency under a lease-acquisition agreement under which the
 2   United States or a Federal agency will receive fee simple title under the terms of such
 3   agreement without further negotiation. Such term also applies to facilities related to programs
 4   administered by Federal agencies.
 5
 6   Federal Emergency Response Official (FERO). A federal agency employee or contractor
 7   who performs functions related to the National Response Framework (NRF), National
 8   Infrastructure Protection Plan (NIPP), or National Continuity Policy Implementation Plan
 9   (NCP/IP). The NCPIP includes Continuity of Operations and Continuity of Government
10   members. Public Law 110-53 applies to incident management personnel, emergency
11   response providers, other personnel, and resources supporting the response to a natural
12   disaster, act of terrorism, or other emergencies
13
14   Logical Access Control System (LACS). Protection mechanisms that limit a user’s access to
15   information and restrict their forms of access on the system to only what is appropriate for
16   them. LACS may be built into an operating system, application, or an added system.
17
18   Non-Federal Employee. A person with a relationship with the USDA other than employment,
19   such as through a contract, grant, agreement, or memorandum of understanding, doing work
20   for or on behalf of the USDA, such as a contractor, affiliate, fellow, intern, or volunteer.
21
22   Physical Access Control System (PACS). Protection mechanisms that limit users' access to
23   physical facilities or areas to only what is appropriate for them. These systems typically
24   involve a combination of hardware and software (e.g., a card reader), and may involve
25   human control (e.g., a security guard)
26
27   USDA LincPass (PIV). USDA has named their common identity card the LincPass, as it is
28   designed to link a person’s identity to an identification card and the card to a person’s ability
29   to access Federal buildings and computer systems. The spelling of LincPass is a tribute to
30   President Abraham Lincoln, who created the Department of Agriculture, also known as the
31   People’s Department, in 1862.
